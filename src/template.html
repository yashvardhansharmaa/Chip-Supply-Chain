How to read a chapter

Every chapter opens with a chokepoint card. Three fields on it matter most.

  • Concentration. How many firms credibly supply this stage: Extreme, High, Moderate or Low.
  • Substitutability. How hard it would be for a well-funded state or firm to stand up an alternative: Easy, Moderate, Hard or Very hard, rated against four written tests. It is the most opinionated field on the card.
  • Price or market size. What one unit costs, or what the stage earns in a year, from filings.

The card also gives China's position and the United States'. The chapters run in process order, and every number links to its source.

AI Chips

The chips that train and run AI models, from the A100 of 2020 to the parts shipping in 2026, with the foundry, process node, memory supplier and packaging plant behind each one. Pick two to four chips: the page sets their specs side by side and draws their packages at one scale. The specimen beside the text follows whichever chip you click.

Packages at one scale

Every package is drawn from above at one scale, so a bigger one looks bigger. The logic dies do the computing, the memory stacks feed them, the interposer is the silicon slab that wires the two together, and the substrate is the card underneath. Click a package to highlight that chip in the table and open its notes.

Specs side by side

Hover any figure to see its source. TFLOPS counts trillions of calculations a second. FP16, FP8 and FP4 are number formats: the fewer the bits, the more calculations a chip gets through.

  • No licenceNone needed.
  • Case by caseA licence is required, and each application is judged on its own.
  • Presumption of denialA licence is required and will normally be refused. An Entity List listing sits here.
  • ProhibitedNo licence to be had, or a flat ban.
  • PolicyMoney and tariffs, not a gate.
  • SuspendedThe instrument is stayed until a date. The outcome beside it returns when the stay ends.
