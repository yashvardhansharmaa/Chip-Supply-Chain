# How the chokepoint matrix was built

This matrix follows a format that CSET, Georgetown's Center for Security and Emerging Technology, established in 2021, when Khan, Mann and Peterson published a single page of country shares by supply chain segment and made the geography of the industry legible for the first time ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/)). Their Table 1 covered eight segments on 2019 data. The Semiconductor Industry Association and Boston Consulting Group did the same for fabrication capacity, first in 2021 and again in 2024 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). This version extends the idea to twenty segments and pulls each one forward to the newest figure available in September 2026.

## Where the numbers come from

Five equipment segments come from CSET's successor project, the Emerging Technology Observatory (ETO) Supply Chain Explorer, which now carries a full country breakdown built from TechInsights data for calendar 2025 and published in July 2026. The explorer draws its charts in the browser, so those five rows cite the underlying provision table directly, which is public and machine-readable ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/georgetown-cset/eto-chip-explorer/main/data/provision.csv)). This is the best data in the table by a wide margin. It gives lithography, deposition, etch and clean, process control and advanced packaging tools at one decimal place, on named market sizes, with a consistent methodology.

Where the explorer has no 2025 data it still carries CSET's original 2019 figures, and it is worth being exact about which. It carries country shares for photomasks (node N33), wafers (N26), EDA software (N84) and core IP (N85). For photoresists (N31) and electronic gases (N91) every share field is blank: CSET's paper broke those markets out by sub-category rather than by country, so there was nothing for ETO to lift. Only the photomask row now rests on those 2019 figures, and its note says so.

Two rows that used to cite CSET's brief for figures the brief does not contain, photoresist and silicon wafers, were re-sourced on 10 September 2026 to Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry, which publishes country shares of twenty-four front-end materials markets for 2021 and 2023 in its semiconductor strategy deck of December 2025 ([source](https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/mono_info_service/joho/conference/semicon_digital/0014/handeji14-4.pdf)). METI takes the shares from Fuji Keizai, a Japanese market-research firm this project cannot cite directly, so both rows are marked `relayed` with `relayedVia: "Fuji Keizai"` under the inherited-numbers rule. Japan's share is printed on each bar; the other columns are measured off the chart's vector geometry, which reproduces every printed label on the slide exactly and sums to 100 on all twenty-four rows.

Assembly, test and packaging comes from the ETO file, at the stage level rather than the node level: ETO gives assembly, test and packaging site capacity by country on a $95bn market in 2022, taken from the Semiconductor Industry Association; SIA and BCG print the same 2022 split in their 2024 report. The EDA, core IP, leading-edge logic and mature logic rows are anchored on the two SIA/BCG value-chain reports, whose 2019 and 2022 exhibits are the only allowed measurements of those segments ([2021](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/BCG-x-SIA-Strengthening-the-Global-Semiconductor-Value-Chain-April-2021_1.pdf), [2024](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). China's position in mature nodes and in domestic equipment comes from CSIS ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). ASML's system counts and revenue come from its own 2025 annual report ([source](https://ourbrand.asml.com/m/71076aaad607de4d/original/asml-2025-annual-report-based-on-us-gaap.pdf)). Where a row rests on a company filing, it cites the filing: the two memory rows come from SK hynix's US prospectus, the core IP row from Arm's Form 20-F and Synopsys's and Cadence's 10-Ks, and the packaging and server rows point at Nvidia's annual report for the supplier structure they can still describe.

## Basis and sources

Six fields on every row say what kind of number it is, without the note having to be read.

`basis` says how the shares were obtained, and takes one of four values.

- `measured`: the cited source states these numbers. Two rows, photomasks and assembly, test and packaging.
- `derived`: computed here from figures the sources state, with the arithmetic in `method`. One row, DUV lithography, which is a revenue-weighted average of ETO's three DUV nodes.
- `relayed`: a Tier A document publishes the number but attributes it to a firm this project cannot cite on its own. Eight rows. `relayedVia` names that firm: TechInsights for four equipment rows, through CSET's analysis; IDC for DRAM and HBM, through SK hynix's prospectus; Fuji Keizai for photoresist and silicon wafers, through METI. The DUV row rests on the same TechInsights data but is marked `derived`, because the weighting across the three nodes is a CHOKEPOINTS calculation. The firm's own publication is still not a source.
- `estimate`: the site's own judgment, with the reasoning in `method` and `note`. Six rows: EDA software, core IP, specialty gases, leading-edge logic, mature logic and AI accelerator design.

`basis` is null on the three rows that carry no shares, as `hhi` and `top2` are. A row with no numbers has no basis for numbers.

`sources` is a list of URLs that contain the row's figures, or that the figures were computed from. It is the field to trust. `source` is the single URL the older build reads; it is kept, and it now holds the first entry of `sources`. Every URL in `sources` was opened and read against the row it supports before it was added, and no URL sits in the list because it is about the segment in general.

`asOf` is the year the shares describe, as a string, and it is not always the year the source was published: the photomask row is `"2019"` data published in 2021, the ATP row `"2022"` data republished in 2024, the two materials rows `"2023"` data published in December 2025. On `estimate` rows `asOf` is the year the estimate is *for*, and `method` names the older measurements it is anchored on. Where `asOf` and the year in the source's title differ, `method` says which is which.

`method` is one or two sentences on how the shares were obtained: the node number, the arithmetic, the anchor. It was pulled out of `note`, which now holds only the judgment calls, the conversions and what the row cannot say.

## Two derived columns

`hhi` and `top2` are computed from `shares` and stored so that nobody has to recompute them.

`hhi` is the Herfindahl-Hirschman index of the row: the sum of the squared percentage shares over the named countries and the Other column, rounded to a whole number. It runs from 10,000 for a monopoly (EUV lithography, the Netherlands at 100) down to 1,756 for the least concentrated row in the table (specialty gases). `top2` is the two largest shares added together, rounded the same way.

Three cautions. Both columns inherit whatever the row's `metric` is, so a revenue-share HHI and a capacity-share HHI are not the same quantity and should not be averaged. Both treat the unattributed Other column as if it were one firm's country, which flatters concentration slightly in the rows that have a large one, ATP at 20 points most of all. And an HHI computed on countries is not the antitrust HHI, which is computed on firms: the EDA row's 5,690 understates a market where two American firms hold most of the American column.

## The rows with no shares

On 9 September 2026 the source rule tightened to Tier A plus four named exceptions, and five rows lost their numbers because the only splits published for them came from market-research firms this project no longer cites. A second, deeper search the same day recovered two of the five. Advanced packaging, IC substrates and server assembly still carry `shares: null`, so no country panel can show them.

The two that came back are DRAM and HBM, and they came from a filing rather than a report. SK hynix registered shares in the United States in July 2026, and its Form 424B4 prospectus prints two charts, `Global DRAM Market Shares by Revenue (includes HBM)` and `Global HBM Market Shares by Revenue`, with actuals for 2024, 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526299963/d32785d424b4.htm)). Both rows use the 2025 actuals converted to country by parent-company headquarters. The prospectus attributes the underlying figures to IDC, and that attribution is the whole reason to be careful here: an IDC report is not citable under this rule, but a registered prospectus is a company filing, and SK hynix has adopted these numbers as its own description of the market it competes in, with securities-law liability attached. Both row notes say so plainly rather than presenting the figures as SK hynix's own measurement.

The three that stayed empty stayed empty for these reasons. For IC substrates, ETO's `Substrates` node names Ibiden, Shinko, Nan Ya, Samsung, Shennan Circuits, Zhuhai Yueya and AKM against four countries and leaves every share field blank, and the White House hundred-day review of June 2021 names the same firms the same way ([source](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/100-day-supply-chain-review-report.pdf)). For advanced packaging, no one measures interposer-based (2.5D) capacity by country; what does exist is a split by buyer. Epoch AI estimates Nvidia at 60.3 percent of world CoWoS capacity in 2025, with Google, AMD and Amazon taking the segment to around 90 percent ([source](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-chip-supply-chain-constraints)). The row's note records it because it is the sharpest published measure of the segment's narrowness, but it is demand, not geography, so it cannot fill the row, and it is itself built on wafer-per-month benchmarks Epoch takes from SemiWiki and TrendForce. For server assembly, none of CSET's brief on big tech in Taiwan, CSIS's Indo-Pacific mapping or Epoch's chip-owner work splits the stage by country, and the contract manufacturers do not break out AI systems.

The outsourced assembly and test row was rebuilt rather than emptied. It used to rank the ten largest such firms by revenue, which was a market-research count and, worse, a headquarters cut that put Amkor's Korean, Philippine and Vietnamese plants in the American column. It now uses ETO's site capacity by country for 2022, which counts by location and includes the assembly that chipmakers and foundries do in-house. The measure changed, so the numbers moved a long way: the United States falls from 15 to 3, and a 20-point unattributed remainder appears that is almost certainly Southeast Asia.

## Converting firm shares to country shares

Most published market data is by company, not by country, so nearly every row required a conversion. The rule here is country of parent-company headquarters, the CSET convention. ASML is NL. Zeiss and Trumpf are DE. Applied Materials, Lam and KLA are US. Tokyo Electron, Lasertec, Hitachi High-Tech, Shin-Etsu and SUMCO are JP. Samsung and SK hynix are KR. TSMC, ASE and Unimicron are TW. ASM International is NL, which is why the Netherlands appears in deposition with an eleven point share that has nothing to do with lithography.

Two conversions are contestable, and the segment notes call them out. Siemens EDA, the design-software arm, is run from Oregon but owned by Siemens AG, so it is assigned to DE; assigning it to the US would move thirteen points and put the US near 87 percent of design software. Arm is headquartered in Cambridge but majority-owned by SoftBank, and is assigned by headquarters, which is why the Core IP row carries a GB column at 48; assigning it by ownership would move those 48 points from GB to Japan. Israel is broken out in the metrology row rather than left in Other, because the six points are two named firms, Nova and Camtek. The Other column in the two materials rows is the source's own unattributed remainder and is not a country: in the wafer row it is where the Chinese suppliers sit, in the photoresist row where the Chinese and any remaining European ones do.

Headquarters is not the same as production location, and where the gap is large enough to mislead the note says so. Micron is American but makes most of its bits in Taiwan and Japan. About a third of the value in an ASML extreme-ultraviolet machine is German, once Zeiss optics and Trumpf lasers are counted. Assembly and test is where the two cuts diverge most, which is why that row moved onto a site-location basis: a headquarters cut erases Southeast Asia, which does about a fifth of the world's assembly.

## Metric

Fourteen segments use revenue share, because that is what the underlying source measures. Three manufacturing segments use capacity share, because the revenue share of a fab depends on what it happens to be making that quarter. Three carry no metric at all. Memory shows why the distinction matters. Through 2026 a price spike lifted industry revenue far faster than bit output, so a revenue share of DRAM says as much about pricing as about who can make what. The DRAM and HBM rows use full-year 2025 actuals rather than a quarter for that reason, and both notes carry the warning; a single-quarter figure from this period would have been a snapshot of a spike.

## Where the numbers are weakest

Six of the seventeen segments that carry numbers are the site's own estimates, and `basis` now says so on each of them. The three with no numbers are absent rather than weak, and the notes above say why. What follows ranks the six, and adds the two rows whose dates are years behind the rest of the table.

Photomasks is the worst. The only clean country split is CSET's from 2019, and it mixes captive mask shops in with merchants, which is why the US shows 40 percent. It also misses the real chokepoint, Hoya's position in extreme-ultraviolet mask blanks. The figure is measured, and six years old.

Specialty gases is next, and it is the only segment left in the table with no country measurement at all. No allowed source splits electronic gases by country as a category. The figures are modeled from the known supplier set, and they understate both Japan and China, which dominate specific molecules rather than the category as a whole: METI, publishing Fuji Keizai's 2023 survey, puts Japan at 92 percent of fluorine gas mixtures and 61 of PFC etch gases, Korea at 47 percent of nitrogen trifluoride and China at 24 percent of it. The row mixes bulk industrial gases with specialty molecules and is good to an order of magnitude.

Mature logic carries the widest error bar of the capacity rows. SIA and BCG measured China at 33 percent of logic capacity at 28 nm and above in 2022. CSIS says China now holds about half of global mature-node capacity, but on a broader definition that includes discretes and analog. The 38 in the table sits between them, and it is a judgment rather than a measurement.

Leading-edge logic is the estimate that matters most, because it is the site's single most-quoted number. The sub-5 nm figure is rolled forward from SIA and BCG's 2022 baseline of Taiwan 69 and South Korea 31 to account for TSMC Arizona, Intel Ireland and Samsung Taylor, because no one publishes a current measurement. Advanced packaging carries no estimate, because its only anchor was a single market-research firm's CoWoS capacity reporting and nothing allowed can take its place. The nearest allowed substitute, the year-end wafers-per-month ladder that would let anyone compute it, sits in Epoch AI's methodology but is quoted there from SemiWiki and TrendForce, so it relays a market-research number rather than an institutional estimate.

EDA software and core IP are estimates for a subtler reason: the segments are measured, but only together. SIA and BCG publish EDA and core IP as one line, 74 percent American in 2019 and 68 in 2022, and the two rows split that line between design software and design IP using the vendors' filed revenue. Synopsys, Cadence and Arm file; Siemens does not break out Siemens EDA, and Imagination, SiFive and the Chinese houses file nothing comparable, so the German column in one row and the denominator in the other are ours. AI accelerator design is an estimate of a market no allowed source publishes as a market at all, and its Chinese column is the softest number in the table.

Two dates lag. The ATP row is 2022, and a search on 10 September 2026 of ETO, both SIA and BCG reports, the 2025 SIA Factbook and SEMI's releases found nothing newer that splits assembly and test capacity by country; the merchant OSATs file their own plant lists, but about half the stage is captive and no one publishes the denominator. The photomask row is 2019 for the same reason. Both rows carry the year in `asOf` and in the note.
