# How the chokepoint matrix was built

This table follows a format CSET established in 2021, when Khan, Mann and Peterson published a single page of country shares by supply chain segment and made the geography of the industry legible for the first time ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/)). Their Table 1 covered eight segments using 2019 data. SIA and BCG did something similar for fabrication capacity, first in 2021 and again in 2024 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). This version extends the idea to twenty segments and pulls each one forward to the newest figure available in September 2026.

## Where the numbers come from

Five equipment segments come from CSET's successor project, the ETO Supply Chain Explorer, which now carries a full country breakdown built from TechInsights data for calendar 2025 and published in July 2026. The explorer is a client-rendered app, so those five rows cite the underlying provision table directly, which is public and machine-readable ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/georgetown-cset/eto-chip-explorer/main/data/provision.csv)). This is the best data in the table by a wide margin. It gives lithography, deposition, etch and clean, process control and advanced packaging tools at one decimal place, on named market sizes, with a consistent methodology. Where the explorer still carries CSET's original 2019 figures, as it does for photoresist, gases and photomasks, that is flagged in the segment note.

Assembly, test and packaging comes from the same file, at the stage level rather than the node level: ETO gives ATP site capacity by country on a $95bn market in 2022, taken from SIA. China's position in mature nodes and in domestic equipment comes from CSIS ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). ASML's system counts and revenue come from its own 2025 annual report ([source](https://www.asml.com/en/investors/annual-report/2025)). Where a row rests on a company filing, it cites the filing: the two memory rows come from SK hynix's US prospectus, and the packaging and server rows point at Nvidia's annual report for the supplier structure they can still describe.

## The rows with no shares

On 9 September 2026 the source rule tightened to Tier A plus four named exceptions, and five rows lost their numbers because the only splits published for them came from market-research firms this project no longer cites. A second, deeper search the same day recovered two of the five. Advanced packaging, IC substrates and server assembly still carry `shares: null` and render as dots.

The two that came back are DRAM and HBM, and they came back from a filing rather than from a report. SK hynix registered shares in the United States in July 2026, and its Form 424B4 prospectus prints two charts, `Global DRAM Market Shares by Revenue (includes HBM)` and `Global HBM Market Shares by Revenue`, with actuals for 2024, 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526299963/d32785d424b4.htm)). Both rows use the 2025 actuals converted to country by parent-company headquarters. The prospectus attributes the underlying figures to IDC, and that attribution is the whole reason to be careful here: an IDC report is not citable under this rule, but a registered prospectus is a company filing, and SK hynix has adopted these numbers as its own description of the market it competes in, with securities-law liability attached. The notes on both rows say so plainly rather than presenting the figures as SK hynix's own measurement.

The three that stayed empty stayed empty for reasons the second search verified rather than assumed. For IC substrates, ETO's `Substrates` node was read row by row: it names Ibiden, Shinko, Nan Ya, Samsung, Shennan Circuits, Zhuhai Yueya and AKM against four countries and leaves every share field blank, and the White House hundred-day review of June 2021 names the same firms the same way ([source](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/100-day-supply-chain-review-report.pdf)). For advanced packaging, no one measures 2.5D capacity by country; what does exist is a split by buyer, and it is good. Epoch AI estimates Nvidia at 60.3 percent of world CoWoS capacity in 2025, with Google, AMD and Amazon taking the segment to around 90 percent ([source](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-chip-supply-chain-constraints)). That is recorded in the row's note because it is the sharpest published measure of the segment's narrowness, but it is demand, not geography, so it cannot fill the row. For server assembly, CSET's brief on big tech in Taiwan, CSIS's Indo-Pacific mapping and Epoch's chip-owner work were all checked and none splits the stage by country; the ODMs do not break out AI systems.

The OSAT row was rebuilt rather than emptied. It used to rank the top ten OSATs by revenue, which was a market-research count and, worse, a headquarters cut that put Amkor's Korean, Philippine and Vietnamese plants in the American column. It now uses ETO's ATP site capacity by country for 2022, which is by location and includes captive assembly inside IDMs and foundries. The measure changed, so the numbers moved a long way: the United States falls from 15 to 3, and a 20-point unattributed bucket appears that is almost certainly Southeast Asia.

## Converting firm shares to country shares

Most published market data is by company, not by country, so nearly every row required a conversion. The rule used here is country of parent-company headquarters, which is the CSET convention. ASML is NL. Zeiss and Trumpf are DE. Applied Materials, Lam and KLA are US. Tokyo Electron, Lasertec, Hitachi High-Tech, Shin-Etsu and SUMCO are JP. Samsung and SK hynix are KR. TSMC, ASE and Unimicron are TW. ASM International is NL, which is why the Netherlands appears in deposition with an eleven point share that has nothing to do with lithography.

Two conversions are contestable and are called out in the segment notes. Siemens EDA is run from Oregon but owned by Siemens AG, and is assigned to DE; assigning it to the US would move thirteen points and put the US near 87 percent of EDA. Arm is headquartered in Cambridge but majority-owned by SoftBank, and is assigned by headquarters, which is why the Core IP row carries a GB column at 48; assigning it by ownership would move those 48 points to Japan. Read Other in the metrology row as Israel, meaning Nova and Camtek.

Headquarters is not the same as production location, and where the gap is large enough to mislead the note says so. Micron is US but fabs most of its bits in Taiwan and Japan. ASML's EUV value is roughly a third German once Zeiss optics and Trumpf lasers are counted. Assembly and test is the segment where the two cuts diverge most, which is why that row was moved onto a site-location basis: on a headquarters cut Amkor's Korean, Philippine and Vietnamese plants land in the American column, and Southeast Asia, which does about a fifth of the world's assembly, disappears entirely.

## Metric

Fourteen segments use revenue share, because that is what the underlying source measures. Three manufacturing segments use capacity share, because the revenue share of a fab is a function of what it happens to be making that quarter. Three carry no metric at all. Memory is the clearest illustration of why the distinction matters. Through 2026 a price spike lifted industry revenue far faster than bit output, so a revenue share of DRAM says as much about pricing as about who can make what. The DRAM and HBM rows use full-year 2025 actuals rather than a quarter for that reason, and both notes carry the warning; a single-quarter figure from this period would have been a snapshot of a spike.

## Where the numbers are weakest

Four of the seventeen segments that carry numbers deserve scepticism. The three with no numbers are not weak, they are absent, and the notes above say why.

Photomasks is the worst. The only clean country split is CSET's from 2019, and it mixes captive mask shops in with merchants, which is why the US shows 40 percent. It also completely misses the real chokepoint, which is Hoya's position in EUV mask blanks.

Specialty gases is next. No public source splits electronic gases by country. The figures are modelled from the known supplier set, and they understate China, which dominates specific molecules rather than the category as a whole.

Mature logic carries the widest error bar. SIA and BCG measured China at 33 percent of sub-28 nm logic capacity in 2022. CSIS says China now holds about half of global mature-node capacity, but on a broader definition that includes discretes and analog. The 38 used here sits between them and is a judgment, not a measurement.

Leading-edge logic is the last CHOKEPOINTS estimate left in the table. The sub-5 nm figure had to be rolled forward from SIA and BCG's 2022 baseline to account for TSMC Arizona, Intel Ireland and Samsung Taylor, and no one publishes a current measurement. Advanced packaging used to sit beside it as a second estimate; it has been emptied instead, because its anchor was a single market-research firm's CoWoS capacity reporting and there is nothing allowed to put in its place. The nearest allowed substitute, the year-end wafers-per-month ladder that would let anyone compute it, exists in Epoch AI's methodology but is explicitly quoted there from SemiWiki and TrendForce, so it is a relay rather than an institutional estimate and is not used.
