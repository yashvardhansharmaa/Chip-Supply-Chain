# Landscape notes

Spec-panel copy for `data/landscape.json`. One section per chip, keyed by `id`. Figures are current to September 2026.

---

## Nvidia B200
`nvidia-b200`

The B200 is the part that made reticle-crossing normal. Nvidia fuses two TSMC 4NP dies, 208 billion transistors in total, with a 10 TB/s NV-HBI link and presents them to software as one GPU ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/)). Eight HBM3E stacks give 192 GB at 8 TB/s, supplied by SK hynix, Micron and Samsung, and the whole assembly sits on a TSMC CoWoS-L interposer, which is why CoWoS-L allocation rather than wafer starts became the constraint on 2024 and 2025 AI capacity. Dense throughput is 2,250 TFLOPS FP16, 4,500 FP8 and 9,000 FP4, derived by halving the sparse figures Nvidia publishes for the eight-GPU HGX B200 baseboard ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/)). Note the two configurations: HGX and DGX boards ship 180 GB per GPU at 1,000 W, while the GB200 NVL72 variant runs at higher power and reaches 10,000 dense FP4 TFLOPS ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-b200/)). Jensen Huang put the module price at $30,000 to $40,000 ([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/19/nvidias-blackwell-ai-chip-will-cost-more-than-30000-ceo-says.html)). Boards and racks are built by Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn and Supermicro.

## Nvidia B300 (GB300 NVL72)
`nvidia-b300`

Blackwell Ultra is the same two-die 4NP silicon as B200 with a different memory stack and a retuned tensor core. Swapping 8-high HBM3E for 12-high lifts capacity from 192 GB to 288 GB at the same 8 TB/s, and it is the single largest reason 12-high HBM3E became the volume product at SK hynix, Micron and Samsung during 2025 ([source](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/18/nvidia_blackwell_ultra/)). Nvidia's own GB300 NVL72 table gives 1,080.2 PFLOPS of dense NVFP4 across 72 GPUs, which is 15,000 dense FP4 TFLOPS per GPU, half again what B200 delivers, while FP16 is unchanged ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/)). The rest of the uplift is in attention: Blackwell Ultra doubles softmax throughput, which matters for long-context inference more than raw matrix rate. Power went from 1,000 W to 1,400 W per GPU, which is what forced liquid cooling to become the default rather than an option, and each GPU now carries an 800 Gb/s ConnectX-8 SuperNIC for scale-out.

## Nvidia Rubin (VR200)
`nvidia-vr200`

Rubin is the first HBM4 part in volume and the first Nvidia GPU where memory, not logic, dominates cost. Two reticle-limited compute dies on TSMC 3 nm carry 336 billion transistors, 224 SMs and 896 sixth-generation tensor cores, joined by NV-HBI and packaged on CoWoS-L ([source](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-rubin-gpu-architecture-powering-the-era-of-agentic-ai/)). Eight HBM4 stacks give 288 GB, and the shipping bandwidth settled at 19.2 TB/s per GPU after suppliers missed the original 22 TB/s pin-speed target, a rare case of memory vendors setting a logic vendor's spec ([source](https://www.techpowerup.com/346983/nvidia-lowers-hbm4-specs-for-vera-rubin-vr200-as-memory-suppliers-miss-22-tb-s-target)). Nvidia qualified all three HBM makers for it, so SK hynix, Samsung and Micron all ship into the same socket. Per-GPU dense throughput is 35,000 TFLOPS NVFP4 and 17,500 FP8, derived from the Vera Rubin NVL72 figures of 2,520 and 1,260 dense PFLOPS across 72 GPUs; Nvidia has not published an FP16 number, and it quotes 50 PFLOPS per GPU for NVFP4 inference with sparsity ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72/)). Racks are reported at $7.8M to $8.8M apiece, roughly $110,000 per GPU once CPUs, NVLink 6 and the chassis are counted, and memory is the line item that moved.

## Nvidia H200 (China-licensed configuration)
`nvidia-h200-china`

This is not a China SKU. It is the standard 2023 Hopper part, an 814 mm2 monolithic die on TSMC 4N with 141 GB of HBM3E at 4.8 TB/s and 700 W ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h200/)). It matters because it is the only Nvidia data center GPU legally shipping into China in 2026. A BIS final rule effective 15 January 2026 moved chips below 21,000 total processing performance and 6,500 GB/s of DRAM bandwidth from presumption of denial to case-by-case review, naming the H200 and the AMD MI325X; the H200's 4,800 GB/s and roughly 15,800 TPP clear both bars ([source](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/bis-revises-license-review-policy-for-advanced-computing-commodities-ai-semiconductors-to-china-and-macau-when-exported-from-the-united-states/)). The rule caps aggregate TPP shipped to China at 50 percent of what goes to US customers and a concurrent presidential proclamation adds a 25 percent tariff. Nvidia arrived here by elimination: the purpose-built B30A, a single die with four HBM stacks at roughly half a B300, was blocked in November 2025 ([source](https://ifp.org/the-b30a-decision/)), and the RTX Pro 6000D was rejected by Beijing in September 2025. First H200 deliveries to ByteDance and Tencent were reported in August 2026, at a small fraction of the legal ceiling.

## AMD Instinct MI355X
`amd-mi355x`

The MI355X is AMD's last eight-GPU-box generation and the one where it matched Nvidia on memory a year early: 288 GB of 12-high HBM3E at 8 TB/s, against the B200's 192 GB ([source](https://www.amd.com/en/products/accelerators/instinct/mi350/mi355x.html)). The package is ten dies, eight accelerator complex dies on TSMC N3P hybrid-bonded onto two N6 I/O dies, 185 billion transistors in total, on a CoWoS-S interposer with the eight HBM stacks flanking the compute. Dense matrix throughput is 2,516 TFLOPS FP16, 5,033 FP8 and 10,066 FP4 across 256 compute units, with structured sparsity doubling each ([source](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/mi355x)). Scale-up is the weakness: seven Infinity Fabric links at 153.6 GB/s each and a full mesh across eight OAM modules, with no rack fabric to answer NVL72 ([source](https://glennklockwood.com/garden/processors/MI355X)). AMD publishes no list price; SemiAnalysis puts 2026 rental at about $1.50 per GPU-hour at hyperscale volume and $2.90 retail.

## AMD Instinct MI455X
`amd-mi455x`

The MI455X is AMD's first genuine rack-scale answer to NVL72, and the first merchant accelerator built on TSMC N2. Twelve dies sit on what AMD calls the largest chip ever built on CoWoS-L: eight N2 accelerator complex dies, two N3 I/O dies and two N3 fabric-and-cache dies carrying 192 MB of L2 at 54 TB/s, 320 billion transistors in total ([source](https://www.storagereview.com/news/amd-mi455x-and-helios-432gb-hbm4-72-gpu-racks-and-a-real-answer-to-vera-rubin)). Twelve HBM4 stacks give 432 GB at 23.3 TB/s, which is 50 percent more memory per package than Rubin carries, and that capacity gap is the entire positioning. Peak throughput is 40.27 PFLOPS MXFP4, 20.13 PFLOPS MXFP8 and 5.03 PFLOPS matrix FP16, and AMD's datasheet keeps a separate structured-sparsity column in which FP16, BF16 and INT8 double to 10.07 PFLOPS while the four- and eight-bit formats gain nothing ([source](https://www.amd.com/content/dam/amd/en/documents/products/accelerators/instinct/amd-instinct-mi455x_brochure.pdf)). The Helios rack puts 72 of them with 18 EPYC Venice CPUs on UALink over Ethernet rather than NVLink, at 260 TB/s intra-rack and 225 to 245 kW, and AMD went to production in July 2026 with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle named as deployers ([source](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1294/aai-2026-amd-delivers-full-stack-compute-for-the-agentic-ai-era)). AMD has not disclosed a per-GPU TDP.

## Google TPU v7 (Ironwood)
`google-tpu-v7-ironwood`

Ironwood is the chip that made a non-Nvidia stack credible at frontier scale. Google's own documentation gives 2,307 TFLOPS BF16 and 4,614 TFLOPS FP8 per chip, 192 GiB of HBM3E at 7,380 GB/s, and confirms the package holds two compute chiplets, each a self-contained device with one TensorCore, two SparseCores and 96 GB of HBM ([source](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/tpu7x)). It went generally available in November 2025 ([source](https://blog.google/products/google-cloud/ironwood-google-tpu-things-to-know/)). The scale-up domain is the headline: 9,216 chips in a 3D torus at 1,200 GB/s per chip, 1.77 PB of shared HBM and 42.5 exaFLOPS FP8, the largest single coherent domain in production ([source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/ironwood-tpu-age-of-inference/)). Supply-chain detail is thin because Google discloses none of it: the node is reported as TSMC N3P, Broadcom is the design partner, HBM allocation between Samsung and SK hynix is disputed between analysts, and the ODM is variously reported as Celestica or Inventec. ServeTheHome's hands-on with the package confirms the eight HBM3E sites visually ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/this-is-the-google-tpu-v7-ironwood-chip/)). No die area, transistor count or TDP has ever been published.

## Google TPU 8i (Zebrafish)
`google-tpu-8i`

With its eighth generation Google split the TPU in two, and the interesting half is the inference chip. TPU 8i is a single larger compute die with 288 GB of HBM at 8,601 GB/s and 10.1 PFLOPS of FP4, paired with 384 MB of on-chip Vmem, three times what the training part carries, specifically to hold KV cache ([source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/tpu-8t-and-tpu-8i-technical-deep-dive)). It drops SparseCores entirely and spends the area on a Collectives Acceleration Engine that cuts on-chip collective latency about fivefold. The fabric changed too: a hierarchical Boardfly topology with a seven-hop maximum instead of a 3D torus, in pods of up to 1,024 chips. The supply-chain point is the design partner: TPU 8t went to Broadcom, but TPU 8i went to MediaTek, which is the first time a Google flagship accelerator has been co-designed by someone other than Broadcom ([source](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/04/24/with-tpu-8-google-makes-genai-systems-much-better-not-just-bigger/)). Google has published no node, packaging, TDP or availability date.

## AWS Trainium3
`aws-trainium3`

Trainium3 is the one large accelerator that deliberately avoids a silicon interposer. It uses TSMC CoWoS-R with an organic thin-film interposer of six copper RDL layers on polymer, mounted on a 20-build-up-layer ABF substrate, which sidesteps the CoWoS-S and CoWoS-L queue that gates everyone else ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/aws-trainium3-deep-dive-a-potential)). Two compute dies on TSMC N3P carry eight NeuronCore-v4 cores and 256 MiB of SBUF, with four 12-high HBM3E stacks giving 144 GB at 4.9 TB/s at 9.6 Gbps pin speed, the fastest HBM3E documented, supplied by SK hynix and Micron after Samsung lost the socket. AWS's Neuron documentation is unusually honest about precision: 671 TFLOPS dense BF16 rising to 2,517 with 4x structured sparsity, 2,517 TFLOPS MXFP8, and 2,517 TFLOPS MXFP4, meaning FP4 buys no throughput at all on this part ([source](https://awsdocs-neuron.readthedocs-hosted.com/en/latest/general/arch/neuron-hardware/trainium3.html)). Trn3 UltraServers went generally available on 2 December 2025 with 144 chips per scale-up domain, 20.7 TB of HBM3E and 362 PFLOPS FP8, linked by NeuronLink-v4 at 2.56 TB/s per device over 160 PCIe Gen6 lanes ([source](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/12/amazon-ec2-trn3-ultraservers/)). Alchip took the back-end physical design from Marvell for this generation.

## Microsoft Maia 200
`microsoft-maia-200`

Maia 200 is the odd one out among 2026 flagships: a single monolithic die rather than a chiplet package. Microsoft puts it on TSMC 3 nm with over 140 billion transistors, 216 GB of HBM3E at 7 TB/s, 272 MB of on-die SRAM, over 10 petaFLOPS FP4 and over 5 petaFLOPS FP8, in a 750 W SoC envelope ([source](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/01/26/maia-200-the-ai-accelerator-built-for-inference/)). The Hot Chips 2026 disclosure put the die at about 820 mm2, just under the single-exposure reticle limit, and gave an effective attention throughput of 1.65 PFLOPS against the 10 PFLOPS peak, which is a useful honesty check on the marketing number ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/microsofts-maia-200-accelerator-at-hot-chips-2026/)). The Next Platform reports the node as N3P, the die as roughly 836 mm2, and SK hynix as the HBM3E supplier across six stacks ([source](https://www.nextplatform.com/2026/01/28/microsoft-takes-on-other-clouds-with-braga-maia-200-ai-compute-engines/)). Networking is all Ethernet with no separate scale-out fabric, 2.8 TB/s of dedicated scale-up per chip across four network planes, four accelerators per tray in a non-switched quad, and clusters up to 6,144. It shipped roughly six months later than Microsoft first planned, into Azure US Central in Iowa first, and Marvell has been the co-design partner.

## Meta MTIA 400
`meta-mtia-400`

Meta announced four MTIA generations at once in March 2026 and says it can now ship a new accelerator roughly every six months ([source](https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-mtia-scale-ai-chips-for-billions/)). MTIA 400 is the second of them: two compute chiplets plus an SoC chiplet for PCIe Gen6 and two networking chiplets, an 8x6 grid of processing elements with a redundancy row, eight HBM3E stacks at 9.4 TB/s and 288 GB, 12 PFLOPS of FP4 and about 6 PFLOPS FP8 at 1,200 W ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/metas-mtia-custom-ai-silicon-at-hot-chips-2026/)). Seventy-two accelerators form one scale-up domain over a switched Ethernet backplane, four to a tray, and the 400, 450 and 500 share a chassis, rack and network so each generation drops into halls that already exist ([source](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/meta-unveils-next-four-generations-of-its-mtia-chip/)). The supply-chain fact that matters is the Broadcom relationship, which Meta confirmed in April 2026: Broadcom supplies the XPU design platform, the advanced packaging and the Ethernet across multiple MTIA generations, with a first phase above one gigawatt ([source](https://about.fb.com/news/meta-partners-with-broadcom/)). That makes Meta the third hyperscaler whose accelerator roadmap runs through a single vendor. Meta has published no node, die area, transistor count or HBM supplier.

## OpenAI Jalapeño
`openai-jalapeno`

Jalapeño is OpenAI's first silicon, a reticle-sized inference ASIC taped out in nine months under the 10 gigawatt co-development deal signed with Broadcom in October 2025 ([source](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-broadcom-announce-strategic-collaboration/)). OpenAI refuses to publish peak FLOPS and argues the metric is wrong, quoting delivered throughput instead: on SemiAnalysis's InferenceX benchmark the part returns about 1,400 tokens per second per user on Kimi-K2.5 and GPT-OSS and over 700 on DeepSeek R1 at concurrency 1 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/openai-jalapeno-better-than-nvidia)). The architecture is memory-sliced, with each core owning a slice of HBM, on the premise that HBM bandwidth is not the bottleneck for inference. The supply chain is entirely Broadcom and its orbit: Broadcom does the design and packaging, Broadcom Tomahawk switches provide the networking, the scale-up protocol runs over Ethernet at 4.8 Tb/s per chip inside a 128-accelerator domain and 1.6 Tb/s across domains, and Celestica builds the boards and racks ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/openai-jalapeno-intelligence-platform-shown-powered-by-broadcom/)). At 700 W against the GB300's 1,400 W, and 1.5x to 1.9x the throughput per kilowatt of Nvidia's GB200 and GB300 racks, it is the first customer-designed part with a credible efficiency win over Nvidia ([source](https://openai.com/index/jalapeno-first-results/)). Node, foundry, die area, HBM capacity and bandwidth remain undisclosed.

## Intel Gaudi 3
`intel-gaudi3`

Gaudi 3 is the only merchant AI accelerator that bet entirely on standard Ethernet. Intel's documentation gives eight matrix multiplication engines and 64 tensor processor cores across two compute dies, 1.8 PFLOPS for both FP8 and BF16, 128 GB of HBM2E from eight stacks at 3.7 TB/s, and 24 200-gigabit RDMA NIC ports on the package ([source](https://docs.habana.ai/en/latest/Gaudi_Overview/Gaudi_Architecture.html)). Twenty-one further on-package 200 Gb/s RoCE links handle scale-up across an eight-card OAM baseboard, so a Gaudi cluster needs no proprietary cable plant and no InfiniBand ([source](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/content-details/817486/intel-gaudi-3-ai-accelerator-white-paper.html)). The absence that killed it is FP4: with no low-precision datapath, Gaudi 3 fell out of contention for 2026 inference work just as the whole market moved to four-bit. It is also the end of the line. Intel cancelled Falcon Shores as a commercial product, its 2026 pipeline is Crescent Island, an Xe3P inference GPU with 160 GB of LPDDR5X rather than HBM, and the rack-scale Jaguar Shores is a 2027 part.

## Cerebras WSE-3 Turbo (CS-4)
`cerebras-wse3-turbo`

Cerebras is the only vendor that ignores the reticle limit rather than working around it. The WSE-3 is one 46,225 mm2 device stitched from 84 reticle-sized tiles on TSMC N5, four trillion transistors and 900,000 AI cores, with 44 GB of on-wafer SRAM and no HBM at all ([source](https://www.cerebras.ai/product-chip)). The Turbo revision doubles clocks to reach 250 PFLOPS, which ServeTheHome's Hot Chips 2026 coverage characterises as sparse FP16, and doubles SRAM bandwidth to 43.2 PB/s ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/cerebras-intros-faster-wse-3-turbo-processor-and-first-rack-scale-cs-4-system/)). That design removes two chokepoints at once, advanced packaging and HBM supply, and replaces them with a yield and power problem: the CS-4 packs three wafers per rack behind a direct-liquid-cooled backpack carrying up to 30 PSU modules, at 750 PFLOPS aggregate and about two microseconds wafer to wafer ([source](https://www.servethehome.com/cerebras-talks-going-rack-scale-with-their-wses-at-hot-chips-2026/)). First CS-4 shipments began in the third quarter of 2026, after Cerebras went public in May ([source](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/introducing-cerebras-cs-4)). Cerebras has never published a per-wafer power figure.

## Groq LPU
`groq-lpu`

Groq's LPU is the purest SRAM-only design in production, a deterministic VLIW pipeline with no external memory: weights, KV cache and activations all live on-die ([source](https://groq.com/lpu)). The first generation was a roughly 725 mm2 die on GlobalFoundries 14 nm with 230 MB of SRAM, fabricated and packaged in the United States, which makes it the only major AI accelerator with no Taiwanese silicon in it, on a raw wafer cost SemiAnalysis estimated below $6,000 against about $16,000 for an H100 wafer ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/groq-inference-tokenomics-speed-but)). The tradeoff is that no single chip holds a useful model, so the unit of deployment is the rack: Groq now quotes 256 LPUs per rack, 128 GB of aggregate SRAM, 40 PB/s of SRAM bandwidth and 315 PFLOPS of FP8, which works out to roughly 500 MB, 156 TB/s and 1,230 TFLOPS per accelerator ([source](https://groq.com/inference)). The 2026 development is that Groq's roadmap product is now an Nvidia-branded rack, the NVIDIA Groq 3 LPX, pairing 256 next-generation LPUs with Vera Rubin. Groq publishes no per-chip TDP, FP16 or FP4 figure.

## Huawei Ascend 910C
`huawei-ascend-910c`

The 910C is the clearest case in the industry of a domestic chip that materially is not one. It is two dies with eight HBM2E stacks on two silicon interposers over a shared organic substrate, nominally SMIC N+2 ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/04/22/news-huaweis-910c-ai-chip-reportedly-gears-up-for-mass-shipment-in-may-amid-u-s-curbs-on-nvidia/)). SemiAnalysis judges that most 910B and 910C dies were actually made on TSMC N7, reaching Huawei through Sophgo before TSMC cut it off, amounting to roughly 2.9 million dies ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ai-cloudmatrix-384-chinas-answer-to-nvidia-gb200-nvl72)). A TechInsights teardown in October 2025 found TSMC, Samsung and SK hynix components still inside the 910C, and China responded by putting TechInsights on its unreliable entity list ([source](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20251005PD200/huawei-ascend-tsmc-techinsights-samsung.html)). Per-chip performance is weak and poorly documented, with analyst figures clustering around 780 to 800 TFLOPS dense BF16 and one 320 TFLOPS outlier that looks like a mislabelled 910B number. Huawei's answer is brute force: CloudMatrix 384 delivers about 300 PFLOPS dense BF16, roughly twice a GB200 NVL72, while drawing about four times the power and being about 2.5 times worse per FLOP.

## Huawei Ascend 950PR
`huawei-ascend-950pr`

The 950PR is the first Huawei accelerator to ship with Huawei's own high-bandwidth memory rather than stockpiled Korean HBM, which is the most consequential claim on the roadmap Xu Zhijun laid out at Huawei Connect in September 2025 ([source](https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2025/9/hc-xu-keynote-speech)). That roadmap promised 950PR in Q1 2026 with HiBL 1.0, 950DT in Q4 2026 with 144 GB of HiZQ 2.0 at 4 TB/s, then the 960 in 2027 and 970 in 2028, alongside an Atlas 950 SuperPoD of 8,192 chips at 8 exaFLOPS FP8. Huawei hit the date: the 950PR launched as the Atlas 350 card in March 2026 and reached mass production in April, quoted at 1.56 PFLOPS FP4 with 112 GB at 1.4 TB/s in 600 W, and claimed at 2.8 times an H20 ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/03/23/news-huawei-debuts-atlas-350-on-ascend-950pr-with-in-house-hbm-touting-2-8x-h20-performance/)). Die-level and card-level figures do not reconcile, with 128 GB at 1.6 TB/s also circulating as the 950PR baseline, and Huawei has never published a package photo, die count or transistor count ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/09/18/news-huawei-unveils-ascend-950-with-in-house-hbm-in-2026-touts-superpod-to-rival-nvidia/)). The part is reported on SMIC N+3 rather than the 910C's N+2, and it is the direct answer to the December 2024 US rule barring SK hynix, Micron and Samsung from shipping HBM2 or newer into China.

## Cambricon Siyuan 590
`cambricon-siyuan-590`

Cambricon is the purest read on the SMIC bottleneck, because unlike Huawei it has no smuggled TSMC die bank to fall back on. The Siyuan 590 entered volume production in the third quarter of 2024 on SMIC N+2, and third-party reporting puts it at roughly 80 percent of an Nvidia A100 ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/12/15/insights-cambricon-remains-chinas-top-ai-chip-startup-rumored-2026-triple-output-faces-smic-limits/)). Beyond that almost nothing is public: Cambricon's own site still lists only the MLU270 and MLU370, with no product page for the 590 or the newer 690 ([source](https://www.cambricon.com/)). The financial ramp is the most dramatic in Chinese semiconductors, from RMB 6.5 billion of revenue and a first profit in 2025 to RMB 6.0 billion in the first half of 2026 alone, with cloud accelerators at 99.98 percent of sales ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/03/09/news-china-gpu-race-intensifies-cambricon-turns-profitable-in-2025-moore-threads-metax-narrow-losses/)). The constraint is the foundry, not demand: N+2 capacity is shared with Huawei and other domestic designers, SMIC cannot expand meaningfully under equipment controls, yields on the 590 and 690 are reported near 20 percent, and the 690 has slipped by about a year. Cambricon has been on the US Entity List since December 2022 and is one of two named launch customers for CXMT's domestic HBM3E.

## Moore Threads MTT S5000
`moore-threads-mtt-s5000`

Moore Threads is the clearest demonstration that Chinese accelerator demand is now policy-driven rather than benchmark-driven. The MTT S5000 is its fourth-generation Pinghu part, in what the company calls large-scale commercial deployment, with a vendor claim of up to 1,000 TFLOPS of dense AI compute and no format named, plus hardware acceleration for Transformer and MoE layers ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/04/28/news-moore-threads-reportedly-turns-profitable-in-1q26-but-remains-loss-making-in-2025-as-rd-hits-rmb-1-3b/)). No node, foundry, memory configuration, TDP or price has been disclosed, and no independent benchmark exists. The company went from a RMB 1 billion annual loss to quarterly profit in about two quarters, on R&D equal to 87 percent of revenue, after listing on the STAR Market in December 2025 ([source](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/10/07/news-chinas-gpu-trio-rise-as-nvidia-retreats-decoding-moore-threads-metax-and-cambricon/)). Its real product is software compatibility rather than silicon: day-zero adaptation to DeepSeek V4 through the FlagOS stack and full Qwen 3.5 support, which is how Chinese accelerators substitute for CUDA in practice ([source](https://technode.com/2026/02/19/moore-threads-completes-full-adaptation-of-qwen3-5-model/)). A dual-chiplet successor called Huashan with eight HBM sites is claimed for 2026, with specifications still undisclosed.
