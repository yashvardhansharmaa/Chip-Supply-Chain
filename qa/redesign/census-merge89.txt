   - The front end of a chip factory is the part that builds the transistors, the billions of tiny switches in every chip. Each switch works like a valve. Current runs along a narrow strip of silicon called the channel, and a small voltage on a gate above the strip opens or shuts the flow. For decades chipmakers made the strips shorter, so the transistors switched faster and more of them fit on a chip. But once the strip gets short enough, the valve stops sealing. Current leaks through even when the gate is off, and the chip burns power doing nothing. The newest kind of transistor, called gate-all-around, cuts this leak by wrapping the gate around all four sides of the strip. Building them takes a long sequence of steps that have to run in exact order. Only three companies, TSMC, Intel and Samsung, make these switches in volume. In 2024 the United States began requiring a license to share the know-how for building them, except with countries that impose matching controls. This chapter covers the know-how for building the newest switches. Chapter 9 covers the factories where advanced semiconductors and AI GPUs are actually built, how many chips they can make and where they are. A chipmaker needs both, and having one does not bring the other. China's SMIC runs factories, but without EUV machines or the know-how for the newest switches, its most advanced chips are 7 nm-class. IBM developed 2 nm technology, but it yields no chips until Rapidus finishes a factory to run it, with mass production planned for 2027.

   - A foundry is a company that makes chips for other companies in its chip factories, or fabs. Those companies send in their designs, and the foundry runs them all through the same building on the same tools. The building costs tens of billions of dollars, and the air inside is cleaner than in an operating room. Even so, each wafer, the silicon disc the chips are built on, moves from machine to machine inside sealed boxes and never touches that air. No chip company sells enough of one product to fill such a factory on its own, so chip companies share a handful of them. TSMC runs the biggest of these shared factories, and all six of its largest plants are in Taiwan. Taiwan also held most of the world's capacity for the most advanced logic chips in 2022. So a political crisis over Taiwan would put most of the supply of those chips at risk. This chapter covers the factories where advanced semiconductors and AI GPUs are actually built, how many chips they can make and where they are. Chapter 8 covers the know-how for building the newest switches inside them. A chipmaker needs both, and having one does not bring the other. Know-how without enough factories makes too few chips, and a new leading-edge factory costs tens of billions of dollars. Today only TSMC, Intel and Samsung have both for the newest chips.
   - 7 nm and below was 79% of TSMC's wafer revenue in 3Q26, on revenue of $46.72 billion at a 66.4% gross margin Source
   - TSMC defines a GIGAFAB as a 300 mm fab with monthly capacity above 100,000 wafers. It operates six, whose combined capacity exceeded 13 million 12-inch equivalent wafers in 2025 Source

   + A chip factory, or fab, builds the transistors, the billions of tiny switches in every chip, and then wires them together. Each switch works like a valve. Current runs along a narrow strip of silicon called the channel, and a small voltage on a gate above the strip opens or shuts the flow. For decades chipmakers made the strips shorter, so the transistors switched faster and more of them fit on a chip. But once the strip gets short enough, current leaks through even when the gate is off, and the chip burns power doing nothing. The newest kind of transistor, called gate-all-around, cuts this leak by wrapping the gate around all four sides of the strip. A chipmaker needs two things to make the newest chips, and having one does not bring the other. The first is the know-how to build the newest switches, a long sequence of steps that have to run in exact order. In 2026 only TSMC, Intel and Samsung had it. The second is factories that cost tens of billions of dollars each, and in 2022 most of the world's capacity for the most advanced logic chips was in Taiwan. China's SMIC runs factories, but without EUV machines or the know-how for the newest switches, its most advanced chips are 7 nm-class. IBM developed 2 nm technology, but it yields no chips until Rapidus finishes a factory to run it, with mass production planned for 2027. No chip company sells enough of one product to fill such a factory on its own, so chip companies share a handful of them, run by foundries that make chips for other companies. TSMC runs the biggest, and all six of its largest plants are in Taiwan.
