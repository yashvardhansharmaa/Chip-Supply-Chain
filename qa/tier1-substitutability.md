# Substitutability: rating all 17 cards

The 1 to 5 score and the substitution-time field are gone. Each card now carries its concentration
band and a Substitutability band, rated against the four tests with one actor: a
well-funded state standing up an alternative today.

## The ranking

Hardest first; within a band, by nearness to the one above.

1. **Lithography**, Very hard. One EUV maker, optics nobody repeated.
2. **Geopolitics**, Very hard. The chain at its narrowest link.
3. **Photomasks**, Hard. One actinic tool; money held it, not physics.
4. **Memory and HBM**, Hard. No fourth firm stacks leading-edge DRAM.
5. **Transistors**, Hard. Three recipes and none for sale.
6. **Foundries**, Hard. Leading nodes yes, gigafabs outside Taiwan no.
7. **Substrates**, Hard. One film at 95 percent, requalified everywhere.
8. **Design and EDA**, Hard. Three flows, none mixing; a fourth is written.
9. **Metrology**, Moderate. A named second source in every family.
10. **Deposition and etch**, Moderate. Three or four vendors a step.
11. **Advanced packaging**, Moderate. Ordinary steps; EMIB seconds them.
12. **Chemicals**, Moderate. Rated on resist; Korea substituted in five years.
13. **Silicon**, Moderate. Five qualified suppliers; requalification is the cost.
14. **Test and assembly**, Moderate. No single-supplier tool anywhere.
15. **Systems**, Moderate. Rated on NVLink and lasers, not racks.
16. **Power**, Moderate. Several vendors and a queue, not a gap.
17. **Component costs**, Moderate. Custom parts ship on the same inputs.

Nothing rates Easy: no stage here has a second source reachable on contracts and months.

**The metrology and photomasks inversion resolves the other way.** Photomasks sits six places higher
because one supplier worldwide sells the actinic inspection tool. Process control has a named second
source in every family, and the chapter calls it a chokepoint of concentration rather than of
physics. Its old 10+ years timed China, an actor barred from buying.

## Biggest moves off the old years

- **Metrology, 10+ to Moderate.** The largest move: the old clock was China's share, not a buyer's.
- **Silicon, 5-10 to Moderate.** Five firms qualify at 300 mm, so the actor requalifies, not invents.
- **Substrates, 5-7 to Hard.** The old range hid a film with no qualified second source.
- **Lithography and photomasks keep their bands, not their reasoning.** Both timed the inventor; on a
  copier's clock photomasks falls to Hard.

## Where the evidence is thin

- **Photomasks and substrates** rest on suppliers' claims about themselves: AGC's count of three,
  Hoya's "exceptionally high", Ajinomoto's 95 percent, Nitto Boseki's "overwhelming advantage".
- **Chemicals, systems and component costs** each hold more than one thing. I rated the hardest input,
  and said so in each `test` line.
- I dropped chapter 14's old 18 to 24 month laser clock. It is nowhere in that chapter.
