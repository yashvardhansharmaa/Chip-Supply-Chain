BIS

The US government office that decides which technologies may be exported, to whom, and on what conditions.

Country Groups

The buckets US export rules sort the world into, published as supplement no.

de minimis

The share of controlled US content a foreign-made item may carry before it becomes subject to the EAR.

denial order

An enforcement order, not a licence policy.

EAR

The body of US rules that governs the export of commercial and dual-use goods, software and technology, written and enforced by the Bureau of Industry and Security.

EAR99

The label for an item that is subject to the EAR but is not listed anywhere on the Commerce Control List, from 15 CFR 734.3(c).

ECCN

The catalogue number that US export rules assign to a controlled item, rather like a customs tariff code for security-sensitive technology.

end-use and end-user controls

Export rules that turn on who the buyer is and what they will do with the item, rather than on what the item is.

Entity List

A published list of foreign companies, institutes and individuals to whom any exporter of an item subject to the EAR needs a licence, not only American suppliers.

foreign direct product rule

A US rule that pulls a foreign-made item under American control when two tests are both met: a product scope, meaning the item is the direct product of specified US technology or software, or was made by a plant that is; and a destination or end-user scope, meaning it is going somewhere or to someone the rule names.

in-country transfer

A change in the end use or the end user of an item inside one foreign country, defined at 15 CFR 734.16.

is-informed letter

A letter from BIS to one named company imposing a licence requirement on a specific export, without any rule being published.

license exception

A written carve-out in export rules that lets a shipment go without an individual licence, if it meets stated conditions.

License Exception ACA

The companion to NAC in 15 CFR 740.8, added in December 2024.

License Exception HBM

The exception at 15 CFR 740.25 for high bandwidth memory under ECCN 3A090.c with a bandwidth density below 3.3 GB/s per square millimetre.

License Exception LPP

An exception created at 15 CFR 740.29 by the AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025 (90 FR 4544), which would have let limited quantities of advanced chips go to most destinations without a licence.

License Exception NAC

The exception at 15 CFR 740.8 that lets certain advanced computing chips ship to Macau, to Country Group D:5 and to entities headquartered there, provided the exporter notifies BIS in advance and BIS does not object.

performance density

A chip's TPP divided by the area of its logic dies in square millimetres, memory stacks excluded.

presumption of denial

A licence review policy, not a ban.

reexport

Shipping an item that is subject to the EAR from one foreign country to another, defined at 15 CFR 734.14.

silicon shield

The argument that Taiwan's dominance of advanced chipmaking deters an attack, because destroying or seizing TSMC would cost an aggressor and the world more than the island is worth.

small yard, high fence

The stated design of US technology controls: restrict the narrow set of items that genuinely decide national security and leave the rest of the trade alone.

subject to the EAR

The test for whether an item falls under US export rules at all.

TPP

The measure US export rules use for a chip's computing power: twice the number of multiply-accumulate operations it does each second, multiplied by the number of bits in each operation, added up across every processing unit on the chip.

VEU

An authorisation BIS grants to a named foreign facility, letting approved items ship to it without an individual licence each time.
