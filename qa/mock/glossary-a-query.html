1 definition

foreign direct product rule

also: FDPR, FDP rule

A US rule that pulls a foreign-made item under American control when two tests are both met: a product scope, meaning the item is the direct product of specified US technology or software, or was made by a plant that is; and a destination or end-user scope, meaning it is going somewhere or to someone the rule names. Both halves are needed. The mechanics are in 15 CFR 734.9.

This is what makes US controls extraterritorial. Without it a listed buyer could order the same chip from a supplier outside America; with it, a chip made in Taiwan on American tools for a listed Chinese firm needs a US licence.
