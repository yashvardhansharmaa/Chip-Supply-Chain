## What the shape of it is

The four fields the site puts in front of readers most often are the cleanest. `suppliers.json` yields six rows out of 669 strings, and five of those six are one small habit: an `aiRelevance` that opens "Another …" or "Part of the cohort …" and so leans on a row the reader may never see, because the Suppliers page filters and the Index card shows one firm alone. The vocabulary list is clean throughout: no *robust*, no *seamless*, no *leverage* as a verb, no *ecosystem*, no *journey*, no *underscores*, no *Importantly* or *Notably*, no triads for rhythm, and one *critical* used as praise in 220 rows. "Leading-edge" is a node, not a compliment, everywhere it appears.

Almost everything worth cutting is in the two places where the data explains itself: `chokepoint-matrix.json` (33 rows out of 20 records) and the notes files. Five shapes, which overlap, carry the whole list:

- **Deixis that only works inside the file**, 39 of the 97 rows: *here*, *above*, *below*, *this page*, *this row*, *the compute row*, *on the site*, *this project*, *this site*. Some of it breaks in the product as well as on the page. `landscape.json`'s "one of four chips on this page" and "the only one on this page" are true of the AI Chips table and false of an Index card, which shows one chip; "the compute row" and "the per-die field above" name the schema rather than anything the reader can see.
- **The row narrating its own revision history**, 15 rows. "This row used to cite CSET's 2021 brief", "The row previously carried an unsourced 2024 split", "Shares withdrawn on 9 September 2026 and still withdrawn" (three times, word for word), "An earlier draft of this entry", "the 450 was in this field until this pass replaced it", "The number that used to sit here". A researcher wants what the row says now and what it rests on; what it said last week is the changelog's job.
- **The data praising its own sourcing**, 14 rows. "Those fields are empty here rather than filled from unofficial reporting" (four times, identical), "We checked the figures against those documents", "reasons the second search verified rather than assumed", "unusually honest", "and say so in the note rather than being quietly rolled forward", "and it is good", and the pair of prospectus defences in the DRAM and HBM rows.
- **The search narrated**, 6 rows: "two searches on 9 September 2026 checked …", "a search on 9 September 2026 of …", "we read ETO's `Substrates` node row by row", "we checked CSET's brief …", "reasons the second search verified", "What the searches did find". In each case the finding survives the cut and only the expedition goes.
- **Instructions to the reader**, 8 rows, all in `chokepoint-matrix.json` and its notes: *Treat the country columns …*, *Treat China's number …*, *Treat the geography …*, *Treat it as one significant figure*, *Read the row as 2022*, *Read that as …*, *Read it knowing what it rests on*, *Read this section as …*. Each is a declarative sentence wearing a hat. Nine further rows are in the first person (*we checked*, *we model*, *we rolled*, *we emptied*, *we do not use*, *the weighting is ours*), all but two of them in `chokepoint-matrix-notes.md`.

Category 2 is nearly empty, and that is the right result: the negative statements in these files are almost all real contrasts about the world. "It does not make leading-edge logic" (Hua Hong), "It never built an EUV system" (Nikon), "It sells silicon IP rather than chips" (Rambus), "Not a China-specific SKU but the standard Hopper part" (H200 China) and "Derived, not a vendor datasheet and not independently measured" (910C, once, as a basis) all earn their negatives. Two do not, and both are in the table: Soitec's `role` says its AI exposure is indirect where the `aiRelevance` beside it already says so, and the matrix notes' "are not weak, they are absent".

Three things I deliberately did not flag, so the next pass does not re-open them:

- **"No allowed source …"**, fifteen instances across `landscape.json` and `chokepoint-matrix.json`. It reads as house jargon, but it is a precise hedge, and every plain-English replacement ("no source publishes", "no published source") makes a claim the site cannot support. The tier vocabulary is already visible on the sources page. Leave it.
- **The superlative-identity opener** on `landscape.json` `notes`: "The first reticle-crossing Nvidia data center GPU", "The purest SRAM-only design in production", "The clearest case …", "The odd one out among 2026 flagships". This is the same template `qa/llm-speak-audit.md` counted at 15 of 18 sections in `landscape-notes.md` and ruled off limits, and it is the file's voice rather than a defect. Worth knowing that *clearest* now carries six chips and one matrix row, and *purest* two chips, so a sixth *clearest* would be one too many.
- **`basis` and `method` attributions**: "A CHOKEPOINTS estimate", "the shares are TechInsights' measurement, relayed by CSET", "share computed from the filed absolute figures", the Korean quotations in the SK hynix rows, and the four bases set out at the top of `exposure-notes.md`. These are the sourcing spine, not prose. The same goes for the 108 `basis` strings in `exposure.json`, which is why that file yields seven rows and five of them are Nova repeating its own `basis` back at the reader.
