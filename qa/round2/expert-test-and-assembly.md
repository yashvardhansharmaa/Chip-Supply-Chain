## 2. Proposals for shared files (exact before and after)

**data/glossary.json, "ATE", `plain`**
- Before: "Automated test equipment, or ATE, is the machine that connects to a finished chip, feeds it millions of test patterns and checks whether the answers coming back are right. Advantest's July 2026 investor guide counts test as the only manufacturing step that puts electrical signals through the chip."
- After: "Automated test equipment, or ATE, is the machine that connects to a chip, on the wafer or in its package, feeds it test patterns and checks whether the answers coming back are right. Advantest's July 2026 investor guide counts test as the only manufacturing step that puts electrical signals through the chip."
- Why: testers work at wafer sort too (Advantest guide July 2026 p. 10: "testers are used for wafer testing and for package testing"), and no source gives "millions".

**"wafer sort", `plain`, second sentence**
- Before: "A probe card lowers thousands of needles onto the pads of one die, and a tester drives signals in and reads the answers."
- After: "Thousands of needles on a probe card press onto the pads of one die, or of several dies at once, and a tester drives signals in and reads the answers."
- Why: the card does not move (see change #15), and one touchdown often covers many dies. Aehr 10-K: wafer probe "can be performed on one or many die at a time, including testing the entire wafer at once."

**"burn-in", `plain`, last sentence**
- Before: "…says its lead AI customer is moving burn-in for its accelerators from the finished system to the wafer."
- After: "…says its lead AI customer is moving all burn-in for its accelerators from the system level to the wafer."
- Why: as change #5.

**"burn-in", `why`**
- Before: "…scraps all of them, so burn-in now protects everything else in the package."
- After: "…scraps all of them, so burn-in on the wafer protects everything else in the package."
- Why: only wafer-level burn-in happens before the parts are joined. A failure in package burn-in still scraps the package.

**"probe card", `why`**
- Before: "…probe card supply and lead time set how fast a new accelerator or memory stack can reach full production."
- After: "…probe card supply and lead time can limit how fast a new accelerator or memory stack reaches full production."
- Why: no source says probe cards set the pace. Wafer, packaging and tester capacity limit a ramp too.

**Animation `wafer-test`.** The captions live in two places: `media/wafer-test.json` `steps[].cap` and the `STEPS` array in `animations/wafer-test.html`. Change both.
- "The wafer". Before: "Every stage so far makes chips. Before any of them…" After: "Earlier stages make the chips. Before any of them…" (Packaging and substrates come before this stage on the site, and they do not make chips.)
- "Needles". Before: "A probe card lowers thousands of needles onto one chip. They press onto small metal pads on its surface." After: "Thousands of needles on a probe card press onto one chip. They touch small metal pads on its surface."
- "Chip by chip". Before: "The tester steps from chip to chip across the wafer. It marks any chip that answers wrong, and that chip goes no further." After: "The wafer moves under the needles, chip by chip. The tester marks any chip that answers wrong, and that chip goes no further." (The prober moves the wafer. The tester only sends and reads signals.)
- "Into the package". Before: "A modern accelerator glues together a dozen expensive pieces…" After: "A modern accelerator bonds together a dozen expensive pieces…" (The pieces are joined with solder and copper, and the chapter itself says "bonded".)
- Optional, in the code: in `sideView()`, keep the card at `CARD_DOWN` and raise the chip row by `(1 - S.c)` times the travel, so the motion matches a prober. The figure note now covers this point in text either way.

## 6. Questions for the owner
1. **Teradyne rounding.** "$2.52 billion", "$1.89 billion" and "$505 million" (body, card, keyFact) are rounded from the 10-K's $2,523.7 million and $1,889,685 and $504,903 thousand. No Teradyne document states them in billions. Earlier this round, ASE's "$20.6 billion" was un-rounded. Should Teradyne get "$2,523.7 million", and should the SoC and memory split be kept or dropped?
2. **"Four of the top ten OSATs are Chinese".** CSET's only source for this (footnote 14) is a Substack, marklapedus.substack.com, which is trade press, and its ranking probably comes from a market-research firm. The line appears in the body, Takeaways, the card's CN note, keyFact 10, flashcard 3 and the OSAT glossary entry. I found no allowed primary ranking. Keep it on CSET's authority, or drop it?
3. **Specimen caption.** "About 305 mm across" and "the real gap is a millimeter or two" come from the specimen's own notes, which cite a consulting blog and a vendor tutorial (neither Tier A or B). I could not confirm either figure from an allowed source. Production probe-card boards for 300 mm probers are often much wider than the wafer. Drop the measurements, or source them?
4. **Grinding and the copper columns.** The glossary entry "dicing" and chapter 10 both say grinding goes on until the copper comes through. In practice the grinder stops just short of the copper and an etch exposes it. I left both alone (I have no allowed source to hand, and chapter 10 is not mine). Align them?
5. **Chart remainder.** The `ate-share` chart's "Teradyne and all others 35" is 100 minus 65, not a figure any source states. Is that acceptable as a chart convention?
