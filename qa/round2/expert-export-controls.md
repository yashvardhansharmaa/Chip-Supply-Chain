## Date convention (my view)
Entries titled "enters into force" or "takes effect" carry the in-force date. The EU 2021 regulation, the EU Chips Act, the Dutch 2023 measure and the Japan 2023 tools entry do this. Every other entry carries the date of the act, with `effectiveDate` when the measure took effect later. The added events follow this, with two exceptions I left as they are:
- **Japan 2025** is dated 28 March, the promulgation date its source gives. The 2019 and 2023 Japan entries use the cabinet-decision date, which for 2025 was 25 March. The deck does not give 25 March, so I did not change it.
- **Italy** is dated 23 November, when Zhejiang Jingsheng disclosed the veto in its filing. Reuters' government sources date the cabinet decision 18 November. I kept the official disclosure date. The owner may prefer 18 November.

I changed two dates: Nexperia, to the act, and the EU control list, to its true in-force date.
