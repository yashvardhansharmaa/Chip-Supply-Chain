## 2. Proposals for shared files (main session to apply)

### Glossary (`data/glossary.json`)
1. **ion implantation**, `plain`. Before: "…The patches they land in then conduct the way the design needs. It is the first step in building each transistor." After: "…The patches they land in then conduct the way the design needs." Why: wrong. A gate-all-around flow starts with Si/SiGe epitaxy and fin patterning. Implants recur throughout the flow (wells, threshold voltage, punch-through stop).
   `why`. Before: "No transistor can be built without it. Implanters are export-controlled. …" After: "Every modern chip process relies on it. Implanters are export-controlled. …" Why: as worded it is an overstatement. Transistors can be doped by diffusion or by in-situ doped epitaxy, which is how nanosheet sources and drains are now made. Every production CMOS flow does still use implantation.
2. **gate-all-around**, `why`. Before: "Only TSMC, Intel and Samsung can build it." After: "Only TSMC, Intel and Samsung make it in volume." Why: Rapidus built 2 nm GAA prototypes in July 2025 (the chapter says so), and IBM and imec build them in research.
3. **node**, `plain`. Before: "…spaces its narrowest wires closer together than Intel's 18A does." After: "…spaces its narrowest wires closer together than Intel's Panther Lake chips on 18A do." Why: 18A supports a 32 nm M0 pitch (SemiAnalysis, 14 Jun 2026). Same fix as change #1.
4. **FinFET**, `why`. Before: "TSMC built its processes on FinFETs until N2, …" After: "TSMC's leading-edge processes used FinFETs until N2, …" Why: TSMC's mature processes are planar. Its FinFETs began at 16 nm (TSMC logic technology page).
5. **leading edge**, `plain`. Before: "…A leading-edge chip is one built on it. Everything older is a mature node." After: "…A leading-edge chip is one built on it." Why: in industry usage "mature" means about 28 nm and older. The 5 and 7 nm nodes are neither leading edge nor mature.
6. **backside power delivery**, `plain`. Before: "The finished wafer is ground thin and the power wires are built on its back. Power arrives through holes drilled up from below, clear of the crowded top." After: "After the transistors and signal wiring are built, the wafer is bonded face down to a second wafer and ground thin, and the power wires are built on its back. Power arrives through tiny holes etched up from below, clear of the crowded top." Why: the wafer is not finished at that point. It is bonded to a carrier (SemiAnalysis Panther Lake teardown: "the bonded carrier"), and the holes are etched, not drilled.
   `why`. Before: "…Intel ships it on its 18A process and TSMC on its A16 process." After: "…Intel ships it on its 18A process. TSMC says A16, its first process with it, will be production-ready in the second half of 2026." Why: TSMC's A16 page (1 Aug 2026 capture) says A16 will be production-ready in 2H26, and its logic page calls A12 the second generation of backside power rail. I found no source showing A16 shipping.
7. **transistor density**, `plain`. Before: "…A14 gives more than 20 percent more logic density than N2." After: "…TSMC says A14, planned for 2028, will give more than 20 percent more logic density than N2." Why: A14 is a plan (TSMC 3228).
8. **nm-class**, `plain`. Before: "…The label is used when a maker's own name for the node is different, or when nothing on it measures 7 nanometers." After: "…The label is used when a maker's own name for the node is different." Why: nothing on any modern node measures its nominal size (the chapter says so), so the clause implies a false exception.

No change needed: CMOS, transistor, front end, back end, SRAM (its "barely shrunk since 5 nm" now matches the body and TSMC's 0.021 µm² cell), pitch. Their cross-chapter claims (Applied's ~$252 million penalty, ch 17; four of the ten largest OSATs in China, ch 11; SemiAnalysis March 2026 front-end bottleneck, ch 11; TSMC July 2026 packaging, ch 11/12; ASM record 2025 revenue, ch 6) are all stated and cited in those chapters.

## 4. Open questions for the owner
1. **SMIC location note** (card `countries`: "SMIC Shanghai and Beijing 300 mm lines"). I found no allowed source that places SMIC's 7 nm-class output in Beijing. Its FinFET capacity is usually reported at SMIC South in Shanghai, and its Beijing 300 mm fabs at 28 nm and older. Should the note keep "Beijing", or does a source back it?
2. **SRAM figure** (change #13). I replaced the 22 percent (N3E to N2, TSMC's claim as SemiAnalysis reported it in 2024) with TSMC's own 2025 paper (1.1 times over the previous node). The two probably use different baselines. To keep the 22 percent as well, date and attribute it ("SemiAnalysis reported in 2024 that TSMC claimed …").
3. **Reuters scanner figures** (change #12). They now read as The Information's, via Reuters. Could someone with Reuters access confirm the article once?
4. **Samsung 2023 forecast** ("high-volume chips … were expected only in 2024", SemiAnalysis, February 2023). It is accurately dated but is an old forecast. The September 2026 sentence on Exynos 2600 (SF2) shows the outcome. Keep it, or replace it with SemiAnalysis's October 2024 view that Samsung's GAA output was still only a low-volume mining chip and a small watch chip?
5. **Glossary alias** "advanced node(s)" points to "leading edge". In the US rules "advanced-node" means 16/14 nm logic and below, which is broader than the leading edge. Keep the alias?
6. Not changed, but a candidate to cut: "Wiring has become as hard to make as the transistor" (BEOL lead) is an unsourced judgment.
