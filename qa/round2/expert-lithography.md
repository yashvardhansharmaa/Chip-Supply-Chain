## 5. Proposals for shared files (exact before and after)

**data/glossary.json** (chapter "lithography"):
1. `immersion lithography`.plain. Before: "Light bends more sharply through water than through air, so the same lens prints finer lines." After: "The water lets the lens focus light at a wider angle than air allows, so the same light prints finer lines." Why: wrong physics. An immersion lens is a different, higher-NA lens; the gain is an NA above 1. ASML lenses-and-mirrors: "Without the water, reaching an NA of more than 1 was impossible. With it, our systems' optics reach an NA of 1.35"; "smaller features with the same wavelength of light".
2. `immersion lithography`.why. Before: "Dutch export licensing covers immersion scanners." After: "Dutch export licensing covers the most advanced immersion scanners." Why: older immersion models are not licensed. ASML 30 June 2023: "Shipments of other ASML systems are not controlled by the Dutch government."
3. `DUV`.why. Before: "So Dutch export licensing draws its line at immersion tools." After: "So Dutch export licensing covers the most advanced immersion tools." Same source.
4. `KrF`.plain. Before: "KrF scanners print the coarser layers of a chip, where lines are far apart, and thick films for packaging." After: "KrF scanners print the coarser layers of a chip, where lines are far apart." Why: packaging lithography is mostly i-line (Canon lineup: "i-line Steppers for Back-End-Of-the-Line (BEOL) and Advanced Packaging Applications"; ASML XT:260, i-line, for advanced packaging, AR p. 17). No source supports KrF here.
5. `lithography`.why. Before: "It is the most concentrated stage in the chain, and the finest layers it prints set how densely transistors and wires pack." After: "Only one company, ASML, makes the machines for its finest layers, and those layers set how densely transistors and wires pack." Why: eight chapter cards are rated "Extreme" and seven of them score 5 (chapters 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12), so "the most concentrated" is an unsourced ranking. The single-maker fact is sourced (AR p. 16).
6. Low priority. `scanner`.plain. Before: "Mask and wafer sweep past each other in a narrow slit of light." After: "Mask and wafer move in step through a narrow slit of light." Why: they sit on separate stages and scan in sync. "Past each other" suggests they touch.
7. Low priority. `i-line`.plain. Before: "i-line is the coarsest lithography light still in use, ..." After: "i-line is the coarsest of the main lithography lights, ..." Why: the entry's own aka lists g-line (436 nm), which some older lines still run.
8. Owner decision. `CSIS`.why ends "..., including $43 billion that China announced for developing its own." The CSIS post (two research interns) gives no source for $43 billion, and I could not confirm it from an allowed source. Consider cutting the clause: "...remained firmly out of China's reach despite massive state investment."
