## 5. Open questions for the owner
1. **IDC HBM shares (open decision).** I found no allowed primary source with a numeric HBM share. Micron's own statement is relative only: prepared remarks, 23 Sep 2025 (10 pp.), p. 4: "our HBM share is on track to grow again, and be in line with our overall DRAM share in this calendar Q3". SK hynix's 6-K gives no HBM share. If IDC goes, a qualitative swap for the card `test` and the oneLiner is CSIS, 11 Dec 2024 (own words): "The global market for HBM is dominated by just three companies: SK Hynix and Samsung of South Korea and Micron of the United States."
2. **The same IDC problem, outside the listed decision.** The body's "Three firms took more than 90 percent of the revenue from … DRAM" (424B4: "according to IDC") and keyFacts[10] (29.1 percent, IDC). CSIS's newer memory piece only repeats TrendForce (90.5 percent, 4Q25), so it is no clean swap.
3. **IFP's 70x and its 2026 projections** use TrendForce for global shipments and SemiAnalysis for CXMT. Keep them (IFP's own estimate), or drop the 70x sentence.
4. **"About 30 micrometers"** (specimenCaption and the animation's Thin step and readout) came from Wikipedia (see the specimens/hbm-stack.js header). I found no allowed source. The figure is right in engineering terms for 12-high cores. A sourced alternative: SK hynix, 26 Sep 2024: "made each DRAM chip 40% thinner than before" to fit 12 layers in the height of 8.
5. **Uncited sentences.** "No other company has both leading-edge DRAM and a stacking line of its own." It is true in my judgment, since CXMT's main node is 1z-class per SemiAnalysis, but it has no citation; cut it or cite it. "As a result, TSMC … makes the base die for SK hynix's HBM4." This rests on the April 2024 MOU ("plans to adopt"), and I found no later SK hynix statement naming TSMC.
6. **The Siemens EDA source** for `hbm-bandwidth` (Tier A) has errors. It says HBM3E is "deployed in the NVIDIA H100" (that is HBM3), gives HBM3E pin speeds of "9.2 to 12.4 Gbps", and calls HBM4 controllers "incompatible", where JEDEC says they are backward compatible. Its three bar values do match primaries: Micron (>1.2 TB/s), JEDEC (2 TB/s) and Samsung (12 Feb 2026: "to a maximum of 3.3 terabytes-per-second"). It stays because a figure takes one source.
7. **`bridge` field**: no build file renders it. "Nothing on the chip itself can fix that" is too strong, because on-chip SRAM cuts memory traffic. Fix it if it is ever shown.
