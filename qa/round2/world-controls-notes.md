# world-controls: non-US measures for the Export Controls timeline

Slug `world-controls`, screenshot port 9798. Only file edited: `data/export-controls-timeline.json` (no notes file exists for it). A copy of the file before any edit is in `qa/round2/scratch/world-controls/timeline-before.json`.

## Conventions learned from the file and the page (20 Oct 2026)
- 57 events, sorted by `date` ascending. Key order: `date`, `effectiveDate` (optional), `actor`, `title`, `summary`, `category`, `instrument`, `frCitation` (optional), `cfr` (optional, US only), `source` (one URL), `gist`, `id`, `flows`.
- `category` must be one of the five names in `Geo.CATS` (src/app.js): US export control, China countermeasure, Allied control, Industrial policy, Corporate. A new name would be colored as a US rule by `lane()`. Non-US, non-China governments all use "Allied control".
- Every "Allied control" entry has `flows: ["allies"]`, including Nexperia (investment-screening) and the EU list. `flows` puts the entry in that board row's history drawer and its event count (check-integrity requires the flow to exist in control-flows.json).
- `instrument` values: rule, entity-listing, denial-order, licence-policy, enforcement, countermeasure, investment-screening, industrial-policy, corporate.
- `frCitation` doubles as the legal citation line under the gist (shown, linked to `source`, only when frCitation or cfr is set). Non-US entries use it for the instrument name (MOFCOM announcement number, Staatscourant, EU regulation number).
- `id` is `YYYY-MM-DD-slug`, stable; the page links entries as `#/controls/<id>`.
- `actor` is "Country (body)".
- Summaries: 3 to 10 sentences, plain, past tense, acronym spelled out on first use, US spelling, "percent".
- check-sources.js fails on any domain not in data/source-tiers.json. Not classified: gov.uk, trade.gov.tw, bmwk.de, governo.it, motie.go.kr. Classified and useful: meti.go.jp, mofcom.gov.cn, english.mofcom.gov.cn, eur-lex.europa.eu, ec.europa.eu, government.nl, pib.gov.in, sec.gov, reuters.com, ft.com, asia.nikkei.com.

Access notes: METI removed its 2019 English releases (the live URLs return a "page not found" with HTTP 403), so the 2019 ones are cited through web.archive.org, as the site already does for METI's 2023 release. Reuters returns 401 to scripts; articles were read through web.archive.org copies and are cited by their reuters.com URL, as the site already does. Evidence below is given as a paraphrase with its location, plus at most one short fragment per source, so the notes reproduce as little copyrighted text as possible. The saved pages under qa/round2/scratch/world-controls/ carry the full wording.

- **Japan, 1 July 2019 (in force 4 July): three items to individual licensing.** VERIFIED (Tier A). METI release "Update of METI's licensing policies and procedures on exports of controlled items to the Republic of Korea", archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20250325151832/https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2019/0701_001.html. Section 2 says that from 4 July exporters must apply for individual licenses for fluorinated polyimide, resist and hydrogen fluoride, because the bulk licenses no longer apply. Section 1 opens public comment on removing Korea from the "white countries". The preamble gives the legal basis, articles 25(1) and 48(1) of the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Act, and says trust "has been significantly undermined".
- **Japan, 2 August 2019 (promulgated 7 Aug, in force 28 Aug): Korea removed from Appended Table III.** VERIFIED (Tier A). METI "The Cabinet Approved Partial Amendment to the Export Trade Control Order", archived at https://web.archive.org/web/20221222221824/https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2019/0802_001.html. Section 1 covers the end of general bulk licenses for Korea, catch-all control, the special general bulk license that stays available, and the key dates. Section 2 introduces Groups A to D: "ROK will be categorized into Group B". Section 3 covers licensing of the three items.
- **Japan, 23 March 2023: three items into the Special General Bulk Export License system.** VERIFIED (Tier A). Already in the bibliography: https://web.archive.org/web/20250408171837/https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2023/0323_001.html. Section 2(1) puts the three items and their technology into the Special General Bulk Export License system. Section 2(2) moves the contact point to METI's bureaus and trade offices. Section 3 reads "Effective as of March 23 (Thu.), 2023."
- **Japan, 27 June 2023 (promulgated 30 June, in force 21 July): Korea added to Appended Table 3.** VERIFIED (Tier A). Live at https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2023/0627_004.html. Section 1 allows general bulk licenses for Korea and removes those exports from catch-all control. Section 3 asks exporters to keep screening. Section 4 reads "Enforcement: July 21 (Fri.), 2023".
- **Taiwan, 10 June 2025: Huawei and SMIC on the strategic high-tech commodities entity list.** VERIFIED (Tier B). trade.gov.tw is not in source-tiers.json, so Reuters (15 June 2025) is the source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/taiwan-adds-chinas-huawei-smic-export-control-list-2025-06-15/ (read via web.archive.org/web/20250616130938). Paragraph 2: exports to listed firms need government approval. Paragraph 4: the economy ministry's trade administration "updated it on June 10" on non-proliferation and security grounds. Paragraph 5: 601 entities added, from Russia, Pakistan, Iran, Myanmar and China. Paragraph 6: its call on manufacturers to verify and assess risks.
- **UK, 16 November 2022: Nexperia ordered to sell at least 86% of Newport Wafer Fab.** VERIFIED (Tier B). gov.uk is not in source-tiers.json, so Reuters is the source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/uk-orders-chinas-nexperia-sell-least-86-microchip-factory-2022-11-16/ (read via web.archive.org/web/20221128122339). Paragraph 1 covers the order and the 86% figure, paragraphs 2 and 3 the law in force since January and its reach back to November 2020, paragraph 5 the compound-semiconductor risk, paragraphs 7 and 8 Nexperia's rejection and appeal, and paragraph 9 the July 2021 move to 100%.
- **UK citation label.** The Reuters article does not name the Act. The frCitation "National Security and Investment Act 2021, section 26 final order" was checked against the government's own notice (read, not cited, because gov.uk is unclassified; assets.publishing.service.gov.uk, NWF_Final_Order_Public_Notice_16112022.pdf, saved in dl-govuk/). Its paragraph 1 states a final order under section 26 of that Act, in force 16 November 2022.
- **Germany, 9 November 2022: Elmos fab sale to Silex vetoed, and ERS Electronic investment blocked.** VERIFIED (Tier B): https://www.reuters.com/markets/deals/germany-block-chinese-takeover-semiconductor-firm-ers-electronic-handelsblatt-2022-11-09/ (read via web.archive.org/web/20231107125835). The slug is from the first version, and the final headline is "Germany blocks Chinese stake in two chipmakers over security concerns". Paragraphs 1 to 3 cover the two decisions, the Silex and Sai Microelectronics ownership, and ERS's Chinese private equity talks. Paragraph 5 is Sai's regret, and paragraph 10 Habeck on semiconductor sovereignty.
- **Korea, 18 September 2019: Japan removed from Korea's white list.** VERIFIED (Tier B): https://www.reuters.com/article/us-southkorea-japan-whitelist/south-korea-removes-japan-from-fast-track-trade-white-list-idUSKBN1W21T2 (read via web.archive.org/web/20190918021538). Paragraph 2: the rules took effect immediately on Wednesday, with longer permit processes for weapons-related exports. Paragraph 3: the trade ministry's statement that this was not retaliation. Paragraph 6: Japan's curbs on three materials "used in chips and display industries". Paragraph 7: Korea's WTO filing on Monday.
- **Italy, 23 November 2021: veto of Zhejiang Jingsheng's purchase of Applied Materials' Italian unit, and the April 2021 LPE veto.** VERIFIED (Tier B): https://www.reuters.com/markets/deals/italys-draghi-vetoes-third-chinese-takeover-this-year-2021-11-23/ (read via web.archive.org/web/20221211172424). Paragraphs 1 and 2 cover the filing and the joint venture. Paragraph 3 gives the 18 November cabinet decision and Giorgetti's semiconductor argument, both from two government sources. Paragraph 5 covers the Singapore and China assets, and paragraph 10 the April veto of Shenzhen Invenland. The April 2021 veto was first reported by Bloomberg, which is not an allowed source, so it appears only inside the November entry.
- **EU, 9 September 2021: Regulation (EU) 2021/821 in force.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_21_4601 (read through the press corner API). EUR-Lex returns an empty bot-challenge page to both fetch tools. Paragraph 1 says the regulation enters into force that day. Paragraph 3 covers pooling expertise on emerging technologies such as advanced computing, and Wassenaar as the basis for many restrictions. The Background section covers the replacement of the 2009 regulation and changes to the list by delegated act. The release links the regulation as OJ L 206 of 2021, the issue that carries Regulation (EU) 2021/821.
- **EU, 21 September 2023: European Chips Act in force.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_23_4518. Paragraph 1 gives entry into force, and paragraph 3 the 20% target for 2030. The "Three Pillars" section gives €3.3 billion of EU funds to be matched by member states, the pilot lines and Chips Fund, first-of-a-kind facilities and state aid, and the monitoring and crisis mechanism. The regulation number 2023/1781 is on the Commission's Chips Act page (digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/european-chips-act).
- **China, 20 October 2023 (in force 1 December 2023): graphite licensing.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/blgg/art/2023/art_f6b1bc49e2c8482f8eb749012bc66dec.html. Item 1 lists the two controlled groups with purity, strength and density thresholds. Item 2 lifts the 2006 temporary controls on other items. Item 3 covers the application route and end-user certificates. Item 9 sets the effective date, 1 December 2023, and repeals 2006 No. 50. The timeline already covers gallium and germanium (2023-07-03), so the brief's condition is met.
- **China, 4 February 2025: tungsten, tellurium, bismuth, molybdenum and indium licensing.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zfxxgk/gkml/art/2025/art_084ded0609404b2d81c746b31c9a03a6.html. Sections 1 to 5 list the items by metal. The indium items, 3C004.a to c, are indium phosphide, trimethylindium and triethylindium. The closing paragraph reads "本公告自发布之日起正式实施". This candidate was not in the brief's list. It was added as the same kind of materials control as gallium (2023) and rare earths (April 2025), and it fills the gap between them.
- **China, 9 October 2025: TechInsights on the Unreliable Entity List.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2025/art_9b662990fa4a4d26ba3984ab5d826960.html. The three numbered measures are the import and export ban, the investment ban, and the ban on dealings and data transfers by parties in China. The annex lists 14 entities, with item 14 being TechInsights and its branches. MOFCOM's 5 November 2025 remarks (art_adc429fe09db443cb2e5837524bc2eda) continue or end only the March and April 2025 UEL measures (Nos. 5, 6 and 7), so No. 10 was not suspended. The MOFCOM text does not say what TechInsights is. Nikkei Asia (10 Oct 2025, paywalled after one paragraph) describes it as a Canadian chip research firm, and adding that would need a second source on the entry.
- **Japan, 28 March 2025 (in force 28 May 2025): advanced chips and more chipmaking tools.** VERIFIED (Tier A): METI briefing deck https://www.meti.go.jp/policy/anpo/law_document/20250328_ristshiryou.pdf (49 pages, Japanese, saved in dl-meti/). Page 2 gives the purpose (preventing military diversion of important and emerging technologies), the Wassenaar-based revisions, and the dates: promulgation 28 March, ordinance 3 April, in force 28 May. Page 15 sets the advanced IC chip thresholds, TPP of 6,000 or more with 600 GB/s or more of transfer rate. The tools are on page 30 (plasma doping ion implanters), 31 (equipment for upgrading DUV immersion tools), 32 (imprint lithography), 33 (etch), 35 (deposition), 36 (annealing), 37 (supercritical CO2 cleaning), 38 (wafer defect inspection), 39 (overlay metrology) and 40 (EUV masks). Page 43 covers computers with advanced IC chips. The English release of the 25 March cabinet decision (meti.go.jp/english/press/2025/0325_005.html) lists only the cabinet-order items, so the deck is the better source.
- **India, 15 July 2026: Semicon 2.0.** VERIFIED (Tier A): https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2284784. Paragraph 1 gives the Rs 1,27,500 crore outlay. The six pillars follow, and the third says the first fab is due to be commissioned in 2028. "Progress of ISM 1.0" lists the twelve units and over Rs 1.64 lakh crore, the unit types, and Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi in production. The 2021 launch (Rs 76,000 crore, PIB PRID=1781723) was also verified. It was not added, because the 2026 release reports the first programme's results.

### Rejected or held back
- **Malaysia, 14 July 2025: permit for US-origin AI chips.** Real and relevant, and verified in MITI's own statement (miti.gov.my PDF, saved in dl-miti/). It makes exports, transshipments and transits of high-performance AI chips of US origin subject to a Strategic Trade Permit with immediate effect, under section 12 of the Strategic Trade Act 2010. Not added because miti.gov.my is not in source-tiers.json, and every archived copy of the Reuters report is Reuters' 401 page. Ready to add once miti.gov.my is classified Tier A (draft below).
- **Korea's 2023 restoration of Japan to its white list.** No allowed, readable source found (Reuters URL not in the archive index, Nikkei paywalled). So the 2019 Korean entry has no 2023 counterpart. Worth adding when sourced.
- **China's dual-use export control regulations (State Council, in force 1 December 2024).** Not added: framework rules, already covered in substance by the 2020 Export Control Law entry, and not chip-specific.
- **China's analog chip anti-dumping case (MOFCOM Announcement 2025 No. 27, opened 13 September 2025).** Real and chip-related, but its status is unclear: the normal deadline of 13 September 2026 has passed and MOFCOM's 2025 and 2026 announcement lists show no extension, ruling or termination. Not added until the status can be stated.
- **China, 2026 measures aimed at Japan and the EU.** Announcement 2026 No. 1 (6 January 2026) bans all dual-use exports to Japanese military users and uses. Nos. 11 and 27 list 40 Japanese defense entities, and Nos. 20 and 30 list 21 EU entities. No. 30 (24 July 2026) includes III-V Lab and Vigo Photonics, but the text does not describe them. None of these names chips or chip materials, so none was added. No. 29 (10 July 2026) is a temporary helium export ban, and its text does not mention chips.
- **Japan, 11 November 2025 cabinet order.** Adds peptide synthesizers, alloy powders and FPGA modules. Minor and not chip-tool specific.
- **Industrial policy in Japan, South Korea (K-Chips Act, 2023) and Taiwan (chips act, 2023).** Same weight as the EU and India entries, but not added in this pass. The Reuters copies could not be read while the archive was unstable. Suggested as a follow-up batch so the industrial-policy lane stays balanced.
