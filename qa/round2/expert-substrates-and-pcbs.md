## 2. Proposals for shared files (exact before and after)

**Animation `substrate` (media/substrate.json and the figure note): no change needed.** Step 4 says "11 layers … on each side of the core in a 2026 AI package substrate, against three on each side for a PC in 1999". The readout says "11 layers / On each side of the core / AI package, 2026". The note says "Three layers of film are drawn on each side of the core" (NL = 8 with the middle two as core, so three film layers on each side). All three match Ajinomoto's June 2026 deck, p. 11, and the chapter. The "high-performance computing" glossary entry already gives the counts per side (11 and 9).

**data/glossary.json**
- `ABF`.plain. Before: "The insulating film inside the base board, the substrate, that a high-end chip package sits on. The board is built up…". After: "The insulating film inside the substrate, the small layered board that the chips in a high-end package sit on. The board is built up…" (rest unchanged). Why: the substrate is part of the package and the chips sit on it. The animation's step 1 says the same.
- `substrate`.plain. Before: "The small, dense circuit board that a chip package sits on, spreading the chip's microscopic connections out to the millimeter-scale solder balls a motherboard can accept. It is built up…". After: "The small, dense circuit board at the base of a chip package. The chips sit on it, and it spreads their microscopic connections out to the millimeter-scale solder balls a motherboard can accept. It is built up…" (rest unchanged).
- `substrate`.why. Before: "A handful of Asian firms build these substrates for others. As packages grow, warping and handling replace the wiring as the limit on what can be built." After: "A handful of firms, most of them in Asia, build these substrates for others. As packages grow, a substrate warps more and needs more layers, so fewer come out working." Why: AT&S and TTM are not Asian. "Replace the wiring as the limit" is an aphorism no cited source states. The new sentence is the chapter's own chokepoint argument.
- `glass cloth`.plain. Before: "…T-glass is a special grade that barely expands when heated, which keeps a large package flat." After: "…which helps keep a large package flat." Why: Nittobo calls T-glass "an effective solution" for warping (p. 17). It reduces warping but does not prevent it alone.
- Optional, `BGA`.plain: add at the end: "In a flip-chip BGA, or FC-BGA, the chip is also mounted face down on the substrate through tiny solder bumps." Why: "FC-BGA" is one of this entry's aliases, and the entry never explains the flip-chip part.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. **Card price slot (open decision; text left as is).** My view as a substrate engineer: the $7.5 billion figure is sound as a number. Prismark is the reference the substrate industry itself plans with, and AT&S states it plainly (p. 33, footnoted "Prismark Partners, 'Application Forecast', January 2026"). Under the policy, though, it is exactly the excluded case: a company report repeating a research firm. Keeping it would make chapter 12 the exception while the Gartner figure in chapter 6 and the IDC figure in chapter 10 are being swapped. **I recommend "Key figure" with Ibiden's investment**: `"costSignal": "Ibiden said in February 2026 that it will invest about JPY 500 billion in high-performance package substrates from fiscal 2026 to fiscal 2028"`, `"costSources": ["https://www.ibiden.com/company/2026/02/notice-regarding-capital-investment-plan-for-high-performance-ic-package-substrates.html"]`, `"costLabel": "Key figure"`. It is the maker's own figure, the body already cites it, and it shows what the capacity race costs. The AT&S sentence "Citing the research firm Prismark…" would then go too. The only other market size I found is SIA/BCG's "$9B" for substrates (May 2024, Exhibit 11, p. 19). It is weaker: it gives no year, and part of it comes from IHS.
2. **Specimen caption vs. the chapter.** The specimen is an 8-2-8 board (18 copper layers) "about 100 by 72 mm". Ajinomoto's slide gives a 2026 AI substrate about 100 mm square 11 layers of film on each side, and even the 2023 HPC substrate had 9 on each side. The caption is true of the drawing, but a reader can set the two side by side. Options: (a) redraw as 11-2-11, or (b) change the caption to "…It is about 100 by 72 mm and 1.5 mm thick. The drawing has eight layers of build-up film on each side of the core, eighteen copper layers in all. Ajinomoto counts eleven on each side in a 2026 AI package." (The specimen's code comment also gives about 40 µm per layer, while the chapter says about 10 µm. Only the comment says this, and readers never see it.)
3. **Add Ibiden's August 2026 glass cloth update?** The chapter now dates the January worry but does not say supply has eased. Proposed sentence, after the dated Apple and Qualcomm sentence in "The materials underneath": "In August 2026 Ibiden said its outlook for glass cloth supply had improved, because it expects a third and fourth supplier to join the two it had used, in Japan and Taiwan ([source](https://www.ibiden.com/ir/items/en_QA_FY2026Q1.pdf))." It could also replace the Apple and Qualcomm line in Takeaways. It needs the bibliography entry in section 2.
4. **KeyFact relevance (optional).** The first keyFact, which the Start-here page shows, is TSMC's "packaging capacity is so tight it now limits customers' growth" (transcript p. 10, verified). That is about TSMC's CoWoS lines, not substrates. An on-topic replacement from the same Ibiden Q&A: "Ibiden said in August 2026 that demand for large, high-layer-count package substrates will continue to significantly exceed the industry's supply capacity".
5. **Same wording in files I do not own.** `data/suppliers.json` (Shinko) also says "a new core plant for advanced chip packages". Align it with change 7 if accepted. `bibliography.json` gives the Shinko notice of 30 Sep 2026 an `accessed` of 2026-09-09, which is before it was published. The chapter's sources were re-read today (2026-10-20).
6. **Intel.** Only trade press reports changes to Intel's glass-substrate plans in 2025 and 2026. The chapter's September 2023 statement stands as written.
