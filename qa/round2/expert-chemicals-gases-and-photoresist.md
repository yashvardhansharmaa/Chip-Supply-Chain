## Questions for the owner
1. **Fuji Keizai through METI (policy).** The 78 percent (oneLiner, `plain`, card US line, keyFact, figure, two flashcards, glossary) comes from a METI chart "prepared based on" Fuji Keizai's survey. That is a government document repeating a market-research firm, which this round's brief asks experts to flag. The chapter discloses the relay in Who makes it, and the precedent from September (the SK hynix prospectus with IDC data, the KLA deck with Gartner data) kept such figures with a label. I recommend keeping it. The only allowed alternative for a country share is CSET's 90 percent, a 2019 Reuters estimate.
2. **Computed remainder.** The `resist-country` stack shows "US, South Korean and other companies 22%". METI prints only Japan's 78 percent, so 22 is computed. Keep it, or ask the main session to render the remainder without a number.
3. **Positive tone only (optional).** `plain` says the wash "takes away the parts the light reached". That is true of positive-tone resists, the most common kind. Fujifilm's 29 Oct 2024 release, already cited, calls negative-tone development (the wash removes the unexposed parts) an industry standard at 193 nm, and the metal-oxide EUV resists work that way too. A clause such as "or, in some resists, the parts it missed" would make it complete at the cost of one more idea in the first paragraph. I left it alone.
4. **Gallium and germanium, upkeep.** China's suspension of the US ban (MOFCOM No. 72) ends on 27 November 2026. The chapter, figure title, card, glossary and flashcard all carry that date and will need a look after it passes.
5. **Scope (optional).** "Disrupted three times since 2019" is the site's own count. China's 2025 export controls on other metals (MOFCOM and GAC announcement 2025 No. 10, 4 February 2025: tungsten, tellurium, bismuth, molybdenum and indium items; No. 18, April 2025: some rare earths) are not covered. Any addition would need its own sentence and sources.
