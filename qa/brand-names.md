# Brand names, titles and domains

Options only; nothing else in the repo changed.

One finding shapes the rest. The chokepoint-map space filled up in 2026, and the closest neighbour is **chokepoints.ai**, live since June, titled "the AI compute stack atlas": ten layers, 6,500 companies. Beside it sit Silicon Analysts' Semiconductor Supply Chain Chokepoint Map, ETO's Supply Chain Explorer, SemiGraph and CSET's 2022 brief *Chokepoints*. This site is not another map. It is one chip walked through fifteen stages with specimens, and the name should say so.

Method: whois at the registry servers, RDAP for .info (its port-43 whois is retired), curl for taken names, a search pass for prior art.

## The fifteen

**Descriptive.**

1. **The Chip Supply Chain.** Clear to all three readers and ownable by none: the category, not a title, so it cannot be cited or headlined. chipsupplychain.com is registered but parked.
2. **Silicon Supply.** Reads like a distributor: a staffer would expect a components business, a journalist would not quote it. siliconsupply.com is a parked lander.
3. **How AI Chips Are Made.** The hero line, and the phrase people search. Warm to a journalist, school-room to a researcher, fine for a staffer, awkward in a footnote. freeCodeCamp has an article of that exact title.
4. **The AI Chip Chain.** Plain, but "chain" alone drops the leverage idea and the initials are unsayable. No prior art.

**The chokepoint idea kept.**

5. **Compute Chokepoints** (current). The audience's own word: GovAI, CSET and RAND all frame chips as a chokepoint. Staffer and researcher hear analysis, not advocacy; journalists like the word. CC is a dead abbreviation. The risk is crowding, not meaning.
6. **Chip Chokepoints.** More concrete, and closer to how the press writes it ("China's chip chokepoints", Defense One). It loses the compute-governance signal the target reader uses.
7. **Chokepoints.** Too big to hold: CSET has a brief of that name, chokepoints.com redirects to a Seattle traffic column, chokepoints.org is live, chokepoints.ai is the rival atlas.
8. **The Chokepoint Map.** Promises a map; the site is a walk with a map on the front. Silicon Analysts already publishes a chokepoint map, so a researcher would confuse the two.

**Coined or metaphor.**

9. **The Silicon Route.** Decodes at once, but the Silk Road echo lands as a claim with a China reader. siliconroute.com is a live shop.
10. **Sand to Rack.** The whole arc in three words, and nobody uses it (the cliché is "sand to silicon"). It reads like a documentary, which costs authority.
11. **Fifteen Stages.** Memorable and true to the structure, but meaningless out of context, and it dates the site.
12. **The Chip Map.** Instantly understood, and the most crowded shelf in the shop: SemiGraph, ETO and Semiconductor Cartography are all chip maps.

**Institutional.**

13. **The Compute Supply Atlas.** Citable and neutral; a staffer would footnote it without blinking. But "atlas" has a queue: compute-atlas.com, chokepoints.ai's "compute stack atlas", SemiconductorX's Bottleneck Atlas, LongYield's Chip Atlas. CSA.
14. **The Chip Supply Atlas.** The same shape with the object instead of the abstraction, warmer, same crowding.
15. **Semiconductor Chain Tracker.** Sounds like a subscription product, promises weekly updates the site does not make, and is long in a URL.

## Titles for the top five

1. **Compute Chokepoints.** Hero: "How AI chips are made" / "Every stage of the supply chain, and who controls it." Browser: `Compute Chokepoints · How AI chips are made`. Masthead: "The AI chip supply chain".
2. **Chip Chokepoints.** Hero: "How AI chips are made" / "Fifteen stages, and who controls each one." Browser: `Chip Chokepoints · How AI chips are made`. Masthead: "The AI chip supply chain".
3. **How AI Chips Are Made.** The name takes the hero's line, so the hero moves. Hero: "Fifteen stages, one chip" / "From polished sand to the rack that runs it." Browser: `How AI Chips Are Made`. Masthead: "Every stage, and who controls it".
4. **The Compute Supply Atlas.** Hero: as now. Browser: `The Compute Supply Atlas`. Masthead: "The AI chip supply chain, stage by stage".
5. **Sand to Rack.** Hero: "How AI chips are made" / "Fifteen stages from sand to the finished rack." Browser: `Sand to Rack · How AI chips are made`. Masthead: "The AI chip supply chain".

The footer credit stands in every option.

## Recommendation

**Keep Compute Chokepoints.** It is the only candidate that speaks the target reader's own vocabulary, and its name space is clean in a way none of the others manage: .com, .org, .net and .ai are all free, rare for a two-word English phrase. Register the .com and .org now, take the .ai too, and point howgpusaremade.com at it. One condition: always write both words. "Chokepoints" alone now belongs to chokepoints.ai, which wins every search for it.

1. **Compute Chokepoints.** Right word, right audience, clean domains, no rename cost, and the wordmark, the PDF, the repo path and the cite block already carry it. Its weakness is the crowd, which the two-word rule and an exact-match domain answer.
2. **Chip Chokepoints.** The safer half-step if the crowding worries you. "Chip" is what the site is about at the object level and what a journalist would type, and every form is free. It reads less like compute governance, more like general interest.
3. **The Compute Supply Atlas.** Best if the goal is to be cited rather than read: it sounds like an institution and survives a footnote. But it joins the atlas queue and over-promises, since an atlas covers everything and this site follows one route well.
4. **How AI Chips Are Made.** The widest reach and the clearest promise, since it is already the hero, but it costs authority with the readers named in the brief and is hard to cite. Good as a domain, weak as a masthead.
5. **Sand to Rack.** The most memorable coined name, and free everywhere. Take it only if the site wants to be read as journalism rather than used as a reference work.

I would not use a name containing "map", or stand on "chokepoints" alone.
