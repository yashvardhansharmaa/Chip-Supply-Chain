# Substrates and PCBs

The silicon interposer wires chips together inside a package. Beyond the interposer, the wiring runs through a build-up substrate made of epoxy and glass, then a printed circuit board of thirty layers, then a server rack.

## How it works

A package substrate is the adapter between a chip and the circuit board under it. The chip's connections are finer than a human hair, but the board is wired in millimeters. The substrate is built in layers, like plywood, around a stiff core of glass cloth and resin. Thin sheets of epoxy film are pressed onto both faces of the core. A laser burns holes through each sheet, and copper plated into the holes carries signals from one layer to the next. Every added layer is another set of holes that can fail. A handful of firms build these substrates. Japan's Ajinomoto dominates the film, and Japan's Nitto Boseki dominates the low-expansion glass cloth in the core.

{{figure:substrate-fanout}}

The substrate maker drills the core and plates it with copper before the build-up starts. In each cycle the maker then presses on a film of epoxy, burns holes through it with a laser to connect the layers, and adds copper wiring on top ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). Each film is about 10 micrometers thick, and a human hair is about 80 micrometers across ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.com/stories/the-ajinomoto-groups-unexpected-role-in-semiconductor-manufacturing-the-insulating-film-abf-born-from-aminoscience)).

Ajinomoto counts 22 layers of its film in a 2026 AI package substrate about 100 mm square, against 18 on a high-performance computing substrate about 70 mm square around 2023 ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). PC substrates had six layers of the film when PCs first adopted it in 1999 ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). Silicon and epoxy swell at different rates when heated, so the larger the package, the more it warps. Nitto Boseki says its low-expansion T-glass cloth is highly rated as an effective fix for that warping, and that its use in substrates has increased significantly ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)).

## Variants and trade-offs

### BT substrates

Substrates built on bismaleimide-triazine laminate, or BT, are the other main family. Mitsubishi Gas Chemical, which makes BT materials, says they go mostly into thin packages for smartphone processors, radio modules and memory ([source](https://www.mgc.co.jp/eng/ir/files/2410022e.pdf)). The company says BT also goes into flip-chip substrates for computer CPUs and GPUs ([source](https://www.mgc.co.jp/eng/ir/files/2410022e.pdf)). In these the chip is mounted face down, and BT laminate forms the core between layers of build-up film ([source](https://www.mgc.co.jp/eng/ir/files/2410022e.pdf)). In an October 2024 investor presentation, the company estimated that its BT resins held about 40 percent of the substrate materials market ([source](https://www.mgc.co.jp/eng/ir/files/2410022e.pdf)).

### ABF build-up substrates

Ajinomoto Build-up Film, or ABF, is the insulating film between the layers of package substrates for logic chips, used in PCs, network equipment, servers and AI equipment ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). Ajinomoto first developed it in 1999 and says major chipmakers have used it as the de facto standard ever since ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/01113/teaserItems1/01/linkList/00/link/3_ICT_E.pdf)). Asked on Ajinomoto's own site whether its share was nearer 30 or 50 percent, the electronic materials division answered, "More like 95 percent market share" ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.com/stories/the-ajinomoto-groups-unexpected-role-in-semiconductor-manufacturing-the-insulating-film-abf-born-from-aminoscience)). It said practically all high-performance computers and servers rely on the film ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.com/stories/the-ajinomoto-groups-unexpected-role-in-semiconductor-manufacturing-the-insulating-film-abf-born-from-aminoscience)). In June 2026 Ajinomoto told investors that the film has held a share of over 95 percent since its launch ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)).

### Glass core substrates

A glass core expands less with heat than a resin core and stays flatter, so chipmakers and substrate makers are both trying it ([source](https://samsungsem.com/global/newsroom/news/view.do?id=9850)).

- **Absolics**, a subsidiary of Korea's SKC, has a final Commerce Department award of up to $75 million for a 120,000-square-foot glass substrate plant in Covington, Georgia ([source](https://www.nist.gov/chips/absolics-georgia-covington)). As of October 2026, the award's project page at NIST, a Commerce Department agency, expected first deliveries to customers in 2025 and production capacity to come online in 2027 ([source](https://www.nist.gov/chips/absolics-georgia-covington)).
- **Intel** announced glass substrates in September 2023 for the latter part of this decade ([source](https://download.intel.com/newsroom/archive/2025/en-us-2023-09-18-intel-unveils-industryleading-glass-substrates-to-meet-demand-for-more-powerful-compute.pdf)).
- **Samsung Electro-Mechanics** signed a joint venture agreement with Sumitomo Chemical's subsidiary Dongwoo Fine-Chem in July 2026 to make glass core substrates ([source](https://www.sumitomo-chem.co.jp/english/news/detail/20260702e_2.html)). The partners plan to have a supply system in place by the second half of fiscal 2027 ([source](https://www.sumitomo-chem.co.jp/english/news/detail/20260702e_2.html)).

## The materials underneath

Ajinomoto's Healthcare and Others segment, which includes electronic materials such as the film, lifted business profit 45.1 percent to JPY 66.2 billion in the year to March 2026 ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/library/result/main/014/teaserItems1/0/linkList/06/link/FY25Q4_Tanshin_E.pdf)). Ajinomoto attributes the rise mainly to higher electronic materials revenue and higher profit in Bio-Pharma Services & Ingredients ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/library/result/main/014/teaserItems1/0/linkList/06/link/FY25Q4_Tanshin_E.pdf)). It has set an investment budget of JPY 25 billion between 2023 and 2030 to expand film production ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). It says there are no concerns about its current supply chain overall ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)). In May 2026 it announced the acquisition of land in Gifu Prefecture for a third production site, scheduled to start operating in 2032 to meet demand after 2030 ([source](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/business_briefing/main/011117/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/20260630_presentation_E.pdf)).

The glass cloth in the core also has a dominant supplier. Nitto Boseki's T-glass is stiff and expands little when heated, and the company calls it essential for making high-density package substrates ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)). Laminate makers such as Mitsubishi Gas Chemical coat the glass cloth with resin and press it with copper foil into the core laminate that substrate makers build on ([source](https://www.mgc.co.jp/eng/ir/files/2410022e.pdf)).

Nitto Boseki claims an overwhelming advantage in quality and capacity for low-expansion glass ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)). It planned about JPY 80 billion of capital spending for fiscal 2024 to 2027, and says market demand seems to have risen about two to three times since that plan was set ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)). In August 2025 it decided to expand glass fiber cloth production at its Fukushima Enterprise Center, with production scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of its fiscal 2026 ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)). Apple and Qualcomm are among the customers worried about Japanese glass cloth supply ([source](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/tech-asia/apple-and-qualcomm-fret-over-strained-supplies-of-japan-s-glass-cloth)).

Nitto Boseki says Chinese and Taiwanese companies are aggressively catching up with it in the special glass sector ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)). Nikkei reports that Chinese chip materials makers are competing with Japanese rivals for a market it sizes at $73 billion ([source](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/tech/semiconductors/china-chip-material-makers-battle-japan-rivals-for-73bn-market)).

## Who makes it

A few Asian companies build substrates for other firms. The White House's 2021 hundred-day supply chain review listed build-up substrate among the materials that commenters called vulnerable ([source](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/100-day-supply-chain-review-report.pdf)). It named key substrate makers including Ibiden, Nanya, Shinko, Samsung, Unimicron, Shennan Circuits, Zhuhai Yueya and AKM, none of them American ([source](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/100-day-supply-chain-review-report.pdf)).

A May 2024 report by the Semiconductor Industry Association and Boston Consulting Group counted the major package substrate vendors by headquarters ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). It found two in Japan, two in Taiwan, one in mainland China and none in the United States ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). TTM Technologies, an American circuit board maker, says it also offers package substrates ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)).

- **Ibiden** will invest about JPY 500 billion in high-performance package substrates from fiscal 2026 to fiscal 2028, starting with about JPY 220 billion, mainly at its Gama plant ([source](https://www.ibiden.com/company/2026/02/notice-regarding-capital-investment-plan-for-high-performance-ic-package-substrates.html)). It planned mass production from fiscal 2027 ([source](https://www.ibiden.com/company/2026/02/notice-regarding-capital-investment-plan-for-high-performance-ic-package-substrates.html)). - **Unimicron** reported NT$42.9 billion of sales in the second quarter of 2026, 52 percent of it build-up substrates and 61 percent of it AI data center work ([source](https://www.unimicron.com/files/money/Earnings/en/2026-Q2-consolidated-en.pdf)).
- **Samsung Electro-Mechanics** grew package solution sales 37 percent year on year to KRW 771.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, on substrates for AI accelerators, server processors and cars ([source](https://m.samsungsem.com/global/newsroom/news/view.do?id=10462)).

In February 2025 a company owned mainly by funds of JIC Capital, a subsidiary of Japan Investment Corporation, launched a tender offer to take Shinko, a Japanese substrate maker, private ([source](https://www.jiccapital.co.jp/en/news/.assets/E_20250217_JIC_JICC_PressRelease.pdf)). The tender offer succeeded in March 2025 ([source](https://pr.fujitsu.com/jp/news/2025/03/en/19-1.pdf)).

In September 2026 Shinko agreed to buy a plant in Mobara, Chiba Prefecture, from Japan Display, a display maker ([source](https://www.shinko.co.jp/english/news/docs/20260930-01_en.pdf)). Shinko said it would make the site a new core plant for advanced chip packages and would also expand capacity at its plants in Nagano ([source](https://www.shinko.co.jp/english/news/docs/20260930-01_en.pdf)). Production at the Mobara plant is scheduled to start in fiscal 2028 ([source](https://www.shinko.co.jp/english/news/docs/20260930-01_en.pdf)).

## The boards under the GPU

TTM Technologies says it regularly builds printed circuit boards of more than 30 layers and can go past 70 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). Those layers carry power to the GPUs and route fast signals between them, so how much signal the laminate absorbs is a main design constraint. Nitto Boseki also makes NE-glass, a low-dielectric glass cloth that absorbs less signal, used in switches and routers in data centers ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf)).

Data center computing was 24 percent of TTM's $2.91 billion of fiscal 2025 revenue, against 14 percent two years earlier ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). TTM says its circuit board competitors are mostly based in China and Taiwan ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). In its annual report for 2025, TTM said it was building a plant in Syracuse, New York, to make ultra-high-density circuit boards for national security needs ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). It expected volume production there in the second half of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). Thailand aims to be the biggest circuit board hub outside China as board makers diversify production ([source](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/tech-asia/thailand-carves-out-a-less-glamorous-ai-niche-printed-circuit-boards)).

## The chokepoint

The few firms that build substrates can add capacity by spending money, and they are spending it. The materials inside the substrate are harder to replace. Ajinomoto dominates the film, and Nitto Boseki dominates the low-expansion glass cloth in the core. Both are adding capacity on plans that run for years.

Packages are also getting bigger. TSMC told investors in July 2026 that its CoWoS packaging roadmap goes beyond 14 times the area a lithography machine can print in one shot ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-08/3e494f0c14dd0890f897aa044415e21d93486cc4/TSMC%202Q26%20Transcript.pdf)). A bigger package warps more and needs more layers, so it has more holes that can break the circuit. That lowers yield, the share of substrates that come out working, and each substrate is already expensive before a chip is mounted on it.

## Key evaluation criteria

- **Layer count** sets how much current and how many signals the substrate can carry. Each added layer is another chance to lose the part.
- **Body size**, the substrate's length and width, affects every other criterion. Once a body is much larger than the chips it carries, warping and handling replace the wiring as the main limit on what can be built.
- **Coefficient of thermal expansion**, how much a material swells when heated, must be close enough to silicon's that the package survives repeated heating and cooling. T-glass in the core provides that match.
- **Dielectric properties**, how much signal the core laminate absorbs, set the speeds the substrate and the board underneath can run.
- **Qualification time** protects the incumbents. Each substrate maker and chip customer has to approve a second supplier's film or laminate before using it.
