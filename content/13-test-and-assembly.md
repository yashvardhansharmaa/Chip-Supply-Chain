# Test and Assembly

Turning silicon into a finished, packaged chip takes 1,000 to 1,500 steps, depending on how complex the chip is, the Semiconductor Industry Association estimated in 2025 ([source](https://www.finance.senate.gov/download/051425-isaacs-testimony&download=1)). Advantest's investor guide of July 2026 says test is the only step that puts electrical signals through the chip ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/investors-guide/Investors_Guide_2607E.pdf)). The earlier stages of chipmaking make dies, the individual chips on a wafer. The back end of the industry, assembly and test, checks which of them work and puts the good ones into packages.

## How it works

Before a chip can be sold, a machine called a tester has to check that it works. While the chips are still on the wafer, thousands of needles press onto small metal pads on each one. The tester sends signals through the needles and reads what comes back, and any chip that fails goes no further. A modern AI accelerator is a dozen expensive pieces bonded into one package, and if one piece is bad, all of them are thrown away. So the industry now tests chips earlier, at higher temperatures and for longer, to make weak ones fail during testing before they reach a finished product. Two firms, Advantest in Japan and Teradyne in the United States, sell most of the testers.

{{figure:wafer-probe}}

Each round of test catches failures the round before it cannot see.

- **Wafer sort.** Thousands of needles land on each die while it is still on the wafer. A tester sends in test patterns and checks the answers, and dies that fail go no further.
- **Burn-in.** The part runs hot and at raised voltage for minutes to days, on the wafer or in the package, so that early-life failures happen in the factory, before the part ships ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1040470/000165495426006919/aehr_10k.htm)).
- **Final test.** The tester checks the packaged part again, because packaging adds failures of its own.
- **System-level test.** The finished part sits in a socket and runs something close to a real workload, because some failures appear only when everything runs at once.

As the parts in a package grow more valuable, finding a bad die before packaging matters more ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/08/aehr-receives-22-million-follow-on-order-for-ai-processor-wafer-level-burn-in-systems/)). Aehr's wafer-level burn-in systems look for early-life and hidden failures before dies are cut from the wafer ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/08/aehr-receives-22-million-follow-on-order-for-ai-processor-wafer-level-burn-in-systems/)). Catching those failures early lowers the risk of packaging high-bandwidth memory and an expensive substrate with a processor that may later fail ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/08/aehr-receives-22-million-follow-on-order-for-ai-processor-wafer-level-burn-in-systems/)). Aehr's lead AI customer is shifting burn-in for its AI accelerators from the finished system to the wafer ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/07/aehr-test-systems-reports-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-financial-results-with-record-quarterly-bookings-and-100-million-effective-backlog/)). The FOX-XP systems that customer ordered from Aehr in August 2026 each burn in nine 300 mm wafers at a time ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/08/aehr-receives-22-million-follow-on-order-for-ai-processor-wafer-level-burn-in-systems/)).

## Variants and trade-offs

### Wafer sort and known-good die

The hard part of wafer sort is the probe card, the circuit board that holds the needles. FormFactor says the high-frequency capability of its probe cards lets it compete favorably in testing high-bandwidth memory, a stack of eight, twelve or sixteen memory dies ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1039399/000103939926000009/form-20251227.htm)). For a large logic die every needle must sit in the same plane, survive repeated touchdowns without drifting, and do it hot. Each card is built for one chip design, so every new design needs a new card and a new qualification.

### Final test, burn-in and system-level test

The bigger the package, the more one bad die can cost. TSMC has told investors that its CoWoS packaging roadmap runs beyond 14 times the area a lithography machine can print in one shot ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-08/3e494f0c14dd0890f897aa044415e21d93486cc4/TSMC%202Q26%20Transcript.pdf)). Every added die is another chance to lose the whole assembly, along with its memory and substrate ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/08/aehr-receives-22-million-follow-on-order-for-ai-processor-wafer-level-burn-in-systems/)). ASE, the largest assembly and test contractor, ran 6,797 testers in the second quarter of 2025 and 8,348 a year later ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122411/000095010326011351/dp250868_6k.htm)).

## Who makes it

Advantest puts its own share at about 65 percent of an estimated $9.0 billion tester market in 2025 ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/result/JE_BIZ_260427_note.pdf)). Its share of system-on-chip (SoC) testers, the machines that test processors and other non-memory chips, rose about ten points in a year to 66 percent ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/result/JE_BIZ_260427_note.pdf)). It held about 60 percent of memory test ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/result/JE_BIZ_260427_note.pdf)).

Teradyne, Advantest's main rival, took $2.52 billion of semiconductor test revenue in 2025, service included ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97210/000119312526059002/ter-20251231.htm)). Of that, $1.89 billion came from SoC testers and $505 million from memory testers ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97210/000119312526059002/ter-20251231.htm)). Teradyne's total revenue grew 13 percent to $3.19 billion in 2025, with fourth-quarter revenue up 44 percent on AI demand ([source](https://investors.teradyne.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/433/teradyne-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)).

{{figure:tester-market}}

The rest of the test and assembly equipment market splits into niches, each with its own suppliers.

- **Probe cards.** FormFactor took $785.0 million of revenue in fiscal 2025 ([source](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001039399/us-gaap/RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax.json)). Technoprobe's revenue grew 15.7 percent to EUR 628.4 million in 2025, about 38 percent of it from AI, and it aims to double capacity by the end of 2027 ([source](https://technoprobe.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/PR-FY-2025_.pdf)).
- **Probers**, the machines that move each wafer under the probe card and keep it at a set temperature. Tokyo Seimitsu, which sells under the Accretech name, says it has the largest global share in probers ([source](https://www.accretech.com/en/ir/index/main/00/teaserItems1/00/linkList/02/link/IntegratedReport2025_E.pdf)). It also makes dicing machines and grinders ([source](https://www.accretech.com/en/ir/index/main/00/teaserItems1/00/linkList/02/link/IntegratedReport2025_E.pdf)). Tokyo Electron makes probers too ([source](https://www.tel.com/product/precio.html)).
- **Handlers and burn-in**, the machines that feed parts into a tester and heat them. Cohu took $453.0 million of revenue in fiscal 2025 ([source](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000021535/us-gaap/RevenueFromContractWithCustomerIncludingAssessedTax.json)). Aehr's revenue was $50.0 million in fiscal 2026, but it booked a record $60.7 million of orders in the fourth quarter alone ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/07/aehr-test-systems-reports-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-financial-results-with-record-quarterly-bookings-and-100-million-effective-backlog/)). It expects revenue of $130 million to $150 million in fiscal 2027 ([source](https://www.aehr.com/2026/07/aehr-test-systems-reports-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-financial-results-with-record-quarterly-bookings-and-100-million-effective-backlog/)).
- **Dicing and grinding.** Dicing saws cut wafers into dies, and grinders thin them. Georgetown University's CSET, analyzing TechInsights data, found that in 2025 Disco held 87 percent and Accretech 8.1 percent of the $1.9 billion market for these machines ([source](https://chipexplorer.eto.tech/?parentNode=N69&selectedNode=N71)). In December 2025 Disco announced a saw that cuts packages up to 400 by 400 mm ([source](https://www.disco.co.jp/eg/news/corp/20251215_1.html)).
- **Bonding.** Kulicke and Soffa took $654.1 million of revenue in fiscal 2025 ([source](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000056978/us-gaap/Revenues.json)). Besi's second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 68.7 percent to EUR 249.9 million on hybrid bonding and data center demand ([source](https://www.besi.com/investor-relations/press-releases/2025/details-1/be-semiconductor-industries-nv-announces-q2-26-and-h1-26-results/)).

Thermocompression bonders use heat and force to press memory dies into a stack and to attach chiplets to substrates. ASMPT says it remained the leader in that market in 2025 and aims for a 35 to 40 percent share ([source](https://www.asmpt.com/en/investor-relations/news-events/asmpt-announces-2025-annual-results/)). In its 2025 results it said it expects the market to reach $1.6 billion by 2028 ([source](https://www.asmpt.com/en/investor-relations/news-events/asmpt-announces-2025-annual-results/)). ASMPT took a repeat order for fifteen chip-to-substrate bonders in December 2025 ([source](https://www.asmpt.com/en/investor-relations/news-events/asmpt-secures-additional-orders-for-fifteen-chip-to-substrate-thermo-compression-bonding-tools-driven-by-ai-tailwind/)). Hanmi Semiconductor in Korea also sells thermocompression bonders, including dual bonders for high-bandwidth memory ([source](https://www.hanmisemi.com/en/product/index.html)). See [Memory and HBM](#memory-and-hbm).

## The back end's geography

Taiwanese firms lead outsourced assembly and test, the OSAT business, and Chinese firms are large challengers. ASE, the biggest, billed $20.6 billion in 2025 ([source](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001122411/ifrs-full/Revenue.json)). About 59 percent of that came from packaging and test and 40 percent from its electronics manufacturing arm ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122411/000095010326001661/dp241159_6k.htm)). CSET counts four of the top ten OSATs by 2024 revenue as headquartered in China ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)).

Malaysia's investment agency said in July 2026 that the country ships 13 percent of the world's packaged chips and wants 7 percent of advanced packaging shipments by 2035 ([source](https://www.mida.gov.my/advanced-packaging-how-malaysia-is-packaging-the-future-of-ai/)). A 2024 study by the Semiconductor Industry Association and Boston Consulting Group gave Malaysia 7 percent of global assembly, test and packaging capacity in 2022, the largest share in Southeast Asia ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). The study measured capacity by factory floor space at contractors and chipmakers' own plants ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)).

ASE spent $2.7 billion on machinery and $1.4 billion on buildings, facilities and automation in the first half of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122411/000095010326011353/dp250875_6k.htm)). It says revenue from leading-edge advanced packaging is running ahead of its $3.5 billion forecast for the year ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122411/000095010326011353/dp250875_6k.htm)). Powertech, another Taiwanese assembly and test firm, aims to run the first panel-level packaging line for AI chips in 2027 ([source](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/tech/semiconductors/powertech-eyes-world-s-first-panel-level-packaging-for-ai-chips-in-2027)).

Amkor, an American assembly and test contractor, took $6.71 billion of revenue in 2025 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1047127/000104712726000007/amkr123125erex-991.htm)). Its revenue in the second quarter of 2026 alone was $1.90 billion, up 26 percent year on year ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1047127/000104712726000043/amkr6302026erex-991.htm)). It has raised the investment in its Arizona campus to $7 billion ([source](https://ir.amkor.com/news-releases/news-release-details/amkor-technology-breaks-ground-new-semiconductor-advanced)).

In October 2024 TSMC and Amkor signed a memorandum of understanding for TSMC to buy packaging and test services from Amkor's planned plant in Peoria, Arizona ([source](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3174)). TSMC would use those services for its customers, especially those using its Phoenix fabs ([source](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3174)). The two announced a long-term partnership in June 2026 ([source](https://ir.amkor.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tsmc-and-amkor-technology-announce-long-term-partnership)).

## The chokepoint

Unlike lithography, no test or assembly tool has a single supplier. Advantest argues that switching to another tester maker means rebuilding the whole test setup used for development, evaluation and production, so market share moves slowly ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/investors-guide/Investors_Guide_2504E.pdf)).

CSET finds that between 2019 and 2024 Chinese toolmakers gained 5 percentage points of share in test tools and 4 points in fabrication tools built for advanced packaging ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)). But they lost share in bonding and other conventional assembly and packaging tools ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)). They still lag significantly behind foreign suppliers ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)).

ASE's packaging and test business took about $12.1 billion from outside customers in 2025 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122411/000119312526135585/d50802d20f.htm)). With Amkor's $6.71 billion, the two billed about $18.8 billion between them, compared with global semiconductor sales of $791.7 billion ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/global-annual-semiconductor-sales-increase-25-6-to-791-7-billion-in-2025/)). That is less than 3 percent of chip sales, for a step every chip has to pass through.

## Key evaluation criteria

- **Test coverage** is the share of hidden failures caught before they reach a package that cannot be taken apart.
- **Test time per part** sets throughput and cost, and large dies, memory stacks and chiplets all push it up.
- **Probe card flatness and life** set the cost of wafer sort. Every needle must stay in the same plane across a full die and hold that over a long life.
- **Thermal control** gets harder as accelerators draw more power. A kilowatt-class accelerator has to be kept at a set temperature in the test socket while it runs at full speed.
- **Capacity and geography** set how many chips can be tested and packaged, and where. Taiwan, China and Malaysia hold most of the floor space, and new capacity takes years.

## Takeaways

Advantest argues that switching to another tester maker means rebuilding a customer's whole test setup, so market share moves slowly ([source](https://www.advantest.com/document/en/investors/ir-library/investors-guide/Investors_Guide_2504E.pdf)). Georgetown's CSET finds that between 2019 and 2024 Chinese toolmakers gained 5 percentage points of share in test tools ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)). Across assembly, test and packaging tools, they still lag significantly behind foreign suppliers ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)). Four of the ten largest chip assembly and test contractors by 2024 revenue are headquartered in China ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/inside-beijings-chipmaking-offensive/)).
