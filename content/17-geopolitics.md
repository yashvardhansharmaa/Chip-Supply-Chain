# Who Controls the Chain

The United States is still changing its export controls, and China is still building its response to them.

## The shape of the contest

An export control is a rule that requires the government's permission for a sale. The government names an item and a country or a buyer. From then on its own companies must hold a license before they ship that item to that country or buyer. The American government extends some of these rules to goods made abroad with American technology. Few governments can use such a rule to much effect, because it works only when their own firms sell something the buyer cannot get elsewhere. The machines, software and chemicals used to make an advanced chip come from a short list of firms in the United States, the Netherlands, Japan and South Korea. China can copy the simpler machines within a few years. It cannot yet copy the machine that prints the finest circuits, or the chemicals that machine needs. It has also yet to show it can make high-bandwidth memory, the stacked memory beside an AI chip, in volume.

In September 2022 National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan said the United States must keep "as large of a lead as possible" in foundational technologies such as advanced logic and memory chips ([source](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefing-room/speeches-remarks/2022/09/16/remarks-by-national-security-advisor-jake-sullivan-at-the-special-competitive-studies-project-global-emerging-technologies-summit/)). The October rules that followed were the first to apply his doctrine of a small yard with a high fence, meaning strict controls on a narrow set of technologies ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/where-chips-fall-us-export-controls-under-biden-administration-2022-2024)). Those rules went beyond the old aim of staying two technology nodes ahead of China and tried to push China's capability back below the level it had already reached ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/choking-chinas-access-future-ai)).

Many license requirements depend on who is buying and why. In October 2018, for example, the Commerce Department put the Chinese chip company Fujian Jinhua Integrated Circuit on its Entity List, so that any US-regulated item needed a license to reach it ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/10/30/2018-23693/addition-of-an-entity-to-the-entity-list)). The 2022 rules went further and required a license for any advanced AI chip, and any tool for an advanced fab, bound for China ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/10/13/2022-21658/implementation-of-additional-export-controls-certain-advanced-computing-and-semiconductor)). Most applications were presumed denied, but equipment for the China fabs of firms headquartered in the United States or allied and partner countries was reviewed case by case ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/10/13/2022-21658/implementation-of-additional-export-controls-certain-advanced-computing-and-semiconductor)).

## The US export-control campaign, 2018 to today

In August 2020 the Commerce Department widened its Foreign Direct Product Rule to require a license for any foreign-made item produced with US technology and bound for Huawei ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/08/20/2020-18213/addition-of-huawei-non-us-affiliates-to-the-entity-list-the-removal-of-temporary-general-license-and)). In 2022 its Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) blocked China's access to the most advanced AI chips and to the tools to make them, and it tightened those rules in 2023 and 2024 ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/where-chips-fall-us-export-controls-under-biden-administration-2022-2024)). In August 2025 the government began licensing some sales of Nvidia's H20 chip to China, and in February 2026 small amounts of its H200 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)).

## Ways around the controls

Renting out time on a chip does not count as exporting it, so a Singapore data center selling time on an H200 already installed there moves nothing across a border ([source](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-734/section-734.13)). The AI Diffusion rule of January 2025 required a license for advanced AI chips bound anywhere in the world ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/15/2025-00636/framework-for-artificial-intelligence-diffusion)). For the data centers it approved, it said that API access to a model, and rented capacity for inference, "are not prohibited" ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/15/2025-00636/framework-for-artificial-intelligence-diffusion)).

The American rules reach that trade through the firms holding the chips. A license under the 15 January 2026 rule for H200-class chips bound for China or Macau requires the applicant to obtain the recipient's know-your-customer procedures ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/html/2026-00789.htm)). It must also obtain the recipient's list of customers renting computing power in China and seven other places, among them Russia and Iran ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/html/2026-00789.htm)). The license also depends on a promise that no algorithm trained on the chips is served to blacklisted or military end users ([source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/html/2026-00789.htm)).

The United States has approved large AI chip sales in the Gulf. In November 2025 Commerce authorized G42 of the United Arab Emirates and HUMAIN of Saudi Arabia to buy the equivalent of up to 35,000 Nvidia GB300 chips each under security and reporting conditions ([source](https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2025/11/statement-uae-and-saudi-chip-exports)). On 10 July 2026 Commerce moved the Emirates into the export-control group that holds close US allies ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14132/enhanced-favorable-treatment-for-the-united-arab-emirates-under-the-export-administration)). It named the Emirati government, G42 and eight US-headquartered AI companies as recipients that need no license for controlled AI chips and systems ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/14/2026-14132/enhanced-favorable-treatment-for-the-united-arab-emirates-under-the-export-administration)).

Smugglers route chips past the controls through Southeast Asia. Since 14 July 2025 Malaysia has required a permit to export, transship or transit any high-performance US-origin AI chip ([source](https://www.miti.gov.my/miti/resources/Media%20Release/%5BFINAL%5D_MITI_Press_Stmt_Malaysia_Regulates_Trade_of_US_AI_Chips_2025-07-14.pdf)). On 25 March 2026 the Justice Department announced charges against three people accused of using Thai companies' names to order servers with export-controlled chips for China ([source](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-national-and-two-us-citizens-charged-conspiring-smuggle-artificial-intelligence)). The orders included one for $170 million that was never completed ([source](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-national-and-two-us-citizens-charged-conspiring-smuggle-artificial-intelligence)).

The House select committee on China cites a median estimate of 140,000 chips smuggled to China in 2024 ([source](https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/media/press-releases/protecting-us-tech-china-committee-and-bipartisan-bicameral-leaders-unite-to-stop-ccp-ai-chip-smuggling)). A bill in Congress, the Chip Security Act, would require location verification on controlled chips within 180 days of enactment ([source](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1705/text)). The House Foreign Affairs Committee approved the House version 42-0 on 26 March 2026 ([source](https://huizenga.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=404259)). It has not become law.

## Allies: the Netherlands, Japan, Korea and Europe

US tool controls alone would only shift the sales to Dutch and Japanese suppliers, so the campaign depends on those two governments. Both have aligned their own controls on chipmaking equipment with the American rules ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/where-chips-fall-us-export-controls-under-biden-administration-2022-2024)).

- **The Netherlands.** The Dutch government began licensing advanced deep-ultraviolet tools on 1 September 2023 and widened the controls in September 2024 ([source](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2024/09/06/the-netherlands-expands-export-control-measure-advanced-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment)). On 1 April 2025 it extended them to measurement and inspection tools ([source](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2025/01/15/klever-export-controls-on-advanced-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-to-be-tightened)).
- **Japan.** Japan's controls on advanced manufacturing equipment took effect on 23 July 2023, apply to all destinations and name no country ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/csis-translation-updated-japanese-export-controls-high-performance-semiconductor)).
- **South Korean fabs in China.** BIS revoked the Validated End-User status that let Samsung's and SK hynix's China subsidiaries and Intel Semiconductor (Dalian) receive US equipment without individual licenses, effective 31 December 2025 ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/02/2025-16735/revocation-of-validated-end-user-authorizations-in-the-peoples-republic-of-china)). BIS said it would license those fabs to keep running, but not to expand capacity or upgrade technology ([source](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-closes-export-controls-loophole-foreign-owned-semiconductor-fabs-china)). SK hynix, which bought the Dalian fab from Intel, received an annual BIS license in December 2025 to ship approved US equipment to its China fabs during 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526299963/d32785d424b4.htm)).

A BIS rule effective 29 September 2025 extended Entity List restrictions to any company at least 50 percent owned by one or more listed firms ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/30/2025-19001/expansion-of-end-user-controls-to-cover-affiliates-of-certain-listed-entities)). On 30 September the Dutch economy minister invoked the Goods Availability Act against Nexperia, a Nijmegen chipmaker that supplies Europe's car industry ([source](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2025/10/12/minister-of-economic-affairs-invokes-goods-availability-act)). He cited governance failures that risked moving technology out of Europe ([source](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2025/10/12/minister-of-economic-affairs-invokes-goods-availability-act)).

Both were suspended in November. On 10 November BIS suspended its rule until 9 November 2026 ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/12/2025-19846/one-year-suspension-of-expansion-of-end-user-controls-for-affiliates-of-certain-listed-entities)). The minister suspended his order on 19 November ([source](https://www.government.nl/documents/2025/11/19/update-on-invoking-goods-availability-act)). Europe holds a chokepoint in leading-edge lithography, but none in the mature-node chips its car industry uses.

The MATCH Act, a bill introduced on 8 April 2026, would ban sales of immersion deep-ultraviolet scanners to China ([source](https://www.foreign.senate.gov/press/rep/release/risch-ricketts-kim-introduce-match-act-level-the-global-playing-field-for-us-tech)). It would also name five Chinese companies, CXMT, Hua Hong, Huawei, SMIC and YMTC, in statute ([source](https://www.foreign.senate.gov/press/rep/release/risch-ricketts-kim-introduce-match-act-level-the-global-playing-field-for-us-tech)). It would give allies 150 days to match those limits before the United States extends the Foreign Direct Product Rule on its own ([source](https://www.foreign.senate.gov/press/rep/release/risch-ricketts-kim-introduce-match-act-level-the-global-playing-field-for-us-tech)).

## China's response

In 2024 China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology reportedly told Chinese telecom operators to strip foreign chips out of their networks by 2027 ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)).

China has also taken measures against American suppliers. Micron, the American memory maker, failed a review by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) on 21 May 2023 and lost its critical-infrastructure buyers in China ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/micron-aggression-right-response-beijings-ban-us-chipmaker)). In September 2025 the CAC reportedly told firms including ByteDance and Alibaba to cancel Nvidia orders ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/newsletter/september-18-2025/)). When the United States licensed H200 sales in February 2026, China restricted them, as it had restricted licensed H20 sales ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) has also restricted exports of minerals.

- **3 December 2024.** MOFCOM banned exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the United States, one day after the American chip rules ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/china-rare-earth-export-ban/)).
- **4 April 2025.** It required export licenses for seven medium and heavy rare earths ([source](https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/Policies/AnnouncementsOrders/art/2025/art_0dd87cbee7b045bf93fabe6ab2faceee.html)).
- **9 October 2025.** It announced licensing, from 1 December 2025, for foreign-made goods containing Chinese rare earths worth 0.1 percent or more of their value ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/t0656_china_rare_earth_controls_2025_61_EN.pdf)). MOFCOM suspended that rule on 7 November 2025 until 10 November 2026 ([source](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2025/art_b1ec77dd3f0d4762952904df7cdaadec.html)).
- **9 November 2025.** MOFCOM suspended its ban on exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the United States until 27 November 2026 ([source](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2025/art_5c68985a6b1a46778e2e8dbff1bb1601.html)). It acted after Donald Trump and Xi Jinping met at the Asia-Pacific summit in October 2025 ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/us-china-trade-truce-has-not-solved-gallium-problem)).

China repealed none of these controls, and the April licensing rules still apply.

Chinese toolmakers held 10 to 15 percent of their home market before 2022, 25 percent in 2024 and 35 percent in 2025 ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). They hold about 40 percent of the Chinese market for etch and deposition tools ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). But in September 2025 the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) judged that making its own extreme-ultraviolet machines remained firmly out of China's reach despite massive state investment ([source](https://www.csis.org/blogs/strategic-technologies-blog/breakthroughs-or-boasts-assessing-recent-chinese-lithography)).

Chinese chipmakers remain behind at the leading edge. SMIC's most advanced process, N+3, reaches the transistor density of TSMC's 6 nm-class process without extreme-ultraviolet scanners ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/steel-smic-n3-teardown)). It prints its tightest layers in several passes on deep-ultraviolet tools ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/steel-smic-n3-teardown)).

In September 2025 the research firm SemiAnalysis conservatively put SMIC's capacity at 7 nm and below at 45,000 wafers a month by the end of 2025 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)). It expected 60,000 in 2026 and 80,000 in 2027 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)). Those figures count wafers, and how many working chips they give depends on yield, the share of chips on a wafer that work.

SemiAnalysis also expected Huawei to ship about 805,000 of its Ascend AI chips in 2025, with dies made by both TSMC and SMIC ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)). It judged that memory would limit China's output of AI chips before logic wafers did ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)). By its count, a stockpile of roughly 13 million memory stacks was enough for about 1.6 million Ascend 910C packages ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)). It expected CXMT, China's own memory maker, to make only about 2 million stacks in 2026, enough for 250,000 to 300,000 more ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)).

Huawei said in September 2025 that its Ascend 950 chips, due in 2026, would carry high-bandwidth memory of its own design ([source](https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2025/9/hc-xu-keynote-speech)). It gave no figure for how much of that memory it would make ([source](https://www.huawei.com/en/news/2025/9/hc-xu-keynote-speech)).

## Industrial policy: CHIPS, Rapidus, Big Fund and the rest

The CHIPS and Science Act authorized about $52.7 billion ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). By July 2025 Commerce had awarded $30.9 billion of direct funding to 19 companies for 40 projects, covering a median 14.2 percent of each project's capital cost ([source](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107882/index.html)). Commerce counted a federal tax credit, the Advanced Manufacturing Investment Credit, when it sized those grants ([source](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107882/index.html)). Since the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025, the credit covers 35 percent of qualified investment in an advanced chip plant placed in service after 2025, up from 25 percent ([source](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107882/index.html)).

On 22 August 2025 the government agreed to convert $5.7 billion of unpaid Intel grants and $3.2 billion of Secure Enclave defense money into $8.9 billion of Intel stock, a 9.9 percent stake ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)).

The United States has also used tariffs to draw chip investment. Under a memorandum signed in January 2026, Taiwanese chip and technology firms are to invest $250 billion in US semiconductor, energy and AI production ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/28/2026-10571/implementing-certain-tariff-related-elements-of-a-trade-and-security-agreement-between-the-american)). In the same memorandum the United States agreed to cap some of its national-security tariffs on Taiwanese goods at 15 percent ([source](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/28/2026-10571/implementing-certain-tariff-related-elements-of-a-trade-and-security-agreement-between-the-american)). On 16 July 2026 TSMC announced another $100 billion for four more Arizona fabs, bringing its total Arizona investment to $265 billion ([source](https://www.phoenix.gov/newsroom/ced-news/tsmc-announces-additional--100-billion-investment-in-arizona.html)).

The European Commission says the EU still depends on other countries for advanced chip manufacturing ([source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-chips-act-20)). On 3 June 2026 it proposed a Chips Act 2.0, which would add measures to support advanced chip production in the EU ([source](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-chips-act-20)).

Japan instead backs a homegrown chipmaker, Rapidus. It began prototyping 2 nm gate-all-around transistors at its IIM-1 plant in July 2025 ([source](https://www.rapidus.inc/en/news_topics/information/rapidus-achieves-significant-milestone-at-its-state-of-the-art-foundry-with-prototyping-of-leading-edge-2nm-gaa-transistors/)). In February 2026 it raised another 267.6 billion yen, about $1.7 billion, from a government agency and 32 companies, aiming for 2 nm mass production by 2027 ([source](https://www.rapidus.inc/en/news_topics/information/rapidus-secures-267-6-billion-yen-in-funding-from-japan-government-and-private-sector-companies/)). By June 2026 it had raised 424.95 billion yen in equity, the latest 150 billion yen from a state agency ([source](https://www.rapidus.inc/en/news_topics/information/20260605-2/)).

China spends more than the United States. The latest round of its National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund, the Big Fund, reached $47 billion ([source](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4012-1.html)). CSIS puts cumulative state funding since 2014 at an estimated $150 billion and calls the CHIPS Act substantially smaller in scale ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)).

## Taiwan

No policy has reduced the world's reliance on Taiwan. Taiwan's foundries held 81.9 percent of the world's contract chipmaking market in 2025, according to the Taiwan Semiconductor Industry Association's August 2026 report ([source](https://www.tsia.org.tw/api/DownloadOverview?ID=36#page=9)). TSMC makes its 2 nm chips only in Taiwan ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)). Taiwan held 69 percent of the world's logic capacity below 10 nm in 2022 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf#page=14)).

Advanced packaging is in Taiwan too. Asked in October 2025 how much CoWoS packaging capacity he would add, TSMC's chairman said only that the company was "working very hard to narrow the gap between the demand and supply" ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf#page=7)). He called wafer and packaging capacity both "very tight" ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf#page=7)).

The silicon shield argument holds that this concentration deters an attack on Taiwan, because so many countries rely on its chips ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)). The Stimson Center calls that open to debate, though many Taiwanese believe it ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)).

With an eye to that shield, Taiwan's rules keep TSMC's newest process at home first, so TSMC's Arizona fabs have run a generation behind its fabs in Taiwan ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)). The Stimson Center expected 2 nm production in Arizona in 2028 at the earliest ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)). TSMC said in October 2025 that it was "preparing to upgrade our technologies faster to N2 and more advanced process technologies in Arizona" ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf#page=5)). The four Arizona fabs it announced in July 2026 are to run at 2 nm or more advanced ([source](https://www.phoenix.gov/newsroom/ced-news/tsmc-announces-additional--100-billion-investment-in-arizona.html)).

The Arizona fabs narrow the gap in wafer production, and TSMC has two advanced packaging fabs planned there ([source](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2025-10/6860312f04fd291d0f26b46c1234f84e6332717e/TSMC%203Q25%20Transcript.pdf#page=13)). But TSMC's work in Taiwan rests on a local supply chain of more than 300 firms, which the Stimson Center judged extremely difficult to copy in the United States ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)). The US push has raised fears in Taiwan that the shield could weaken ([source](https://www.stimson.org/2025/why-taiwan-fears-america-first-risks-eroding-its-silicon-shield/)).

## Who gains and who pays

- **Taiwan and Korea.** None of these policies will move leading-edge logic, memory stacking or advanced packaging out of Taiwan and Korea before 2030. Reshoring adds capacity elsewhere without taking any away from them.
- **The Netherlands and Japan.** Their toolmakers build the controlled machines, so the two governments gain a say in who gets them. China accounts for a falling share of the firms' sales. At ASML, China's share of net system sales, which leave out service and field options, fell from 41 percent in 2024 to 33 percent in 2025 ([source](https://ourbrand.asml.com/m/3136300aa4999bc1/original/2026_01_28_Presentation-Investor-Relations-Q4-2025.pdf#page=9)). Against total net sales, the measure in the chart, it fell from 36.1 percent to 29.1 percent ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026011378/R71.htm)). At Tokyo Electron, China's share of sales fell from 44.4 percent in the year to March 2024 to 34.1 percent in the year to March 2026 ([source](https://www.tel.com/ir/library/report/pjuomj00000000tf-att/fy26q4transcript-e.pdf#page=27)). The company said China's share of its sales dropped to 26.8 percent in the quarter to March 2026 because spending on leading-edge chips grew faster than spending on older ones ([source](https://www.tel.com/ir/library/report/pjuomj00000000tf-att/fy26q4transcript-e.pdf#page=5)).
- **The United States.** Its firms took 65 percent of the world's logic chip sales in 2022 ([source](https://chipexplorer.eto.tech/?parentNode=N0&selectedNode=N6)). The Semiconductor Industry Association, using data from World Semiconductor Trade Statistics, Gartner and Omdia, put US firms' share of all chip sales in 2025 at 53.4 percent, the highest since 1984 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SIA_2026_State-of-the-Semiconductor-Industry.pdf#page=7)). Commerce expects the CHIPS-funded projects to lift the US share of global leading-edge logic chip manufacturing from 0 percent in 2022 to 20 percent by 2030 ([source](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107882/index.html)).
- **China.** Mature-node chips, tools below the leading edge, DRAM and NAND are moving to Chinese suppliers. In September 2025 SemiAnalysis expected China, without smuggled memory, to make fewer Ascend chips in 2026 than in 2025 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)).

## Takeaways

Japan and the Netherlands have aligned their own export controls with the American rules on chipmaking equipment ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/where-chips-fall-us-export-controls-under-biden-administration-2022-2024)). Chinese toolmakers supplied 35 percent of China's own chipmaking equipment market in 2025, up from 10 to 15 percent before 2022 ([source](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-localization-drive-semiconductors-gains-impetus-allied-chip-export-controls)). When the United States licensed sales of Nvidia's H200 chip in February 2026, China restricted them ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).

The most advanced chips come mainly from Taiwan. It held 69 percent of the world's capacity for logic chips below 10 nanometers in 2022, by the count of the Semiconductor Industry Association and Boston Consulting Group ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf#page=14)).
