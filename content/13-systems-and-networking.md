# Systems and Networking

Before a chip package can compute, it has to be built into a machine. Building that machine is a separate industry from making the package.

## How it works

One AI chip is not enough to train a model. So seventy-two of them are wired together in a rack, a cabinet the size of a wardrobe, and run as one computer. Many such racks are then linked to train a large model. Each chip sits on its own board and draws more than a thousand amps, several times the current a whole house is wired to carry. The chips have to swap results with each other all the time. A copper wire can carry those signals only a few meters before they fade, so the longer links send them as light down glass fiber. Many companies can build the rack, but in 2026 the lasers that send the light were scarce.

{{figure:racks-wired}}

An accelerator (the AI chip) ships as a module, the chip package on a small board with the circuits that feed it power. Nvidia calls its version SXM, and the open-standards equivalent is the Open Compute Project's accelerator module. Eight modules bolt onto a baseboard that carries their power and the links between them. On Nvidia's baseboard, switch chips pass data between any two modules at full speed.

Rack-scale systems drop the baseboard and put the chips on trays stacked in the rack. An Nvidia GB300 NVL72 holds 18 compute trays plus nine switch trays, 72 GPUs in all, and draws up to 142 kW ([source](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/nvl72-ai-factory/latest/components.html)). The newer Vera Rubin NVL72 uses the same rack footprint ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/gigawatt-ai-factories-ocp-vera-rubin)). The research firm SemiAnalysis estimated in August 2025 that a rack of the earlier GB200 NVL72 model cost a typical hyperscaler $3.1 million, or about $3.9 million with networking, storage and other items ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/h100-vs-gb200-nvl72-training-benchmarks)).

Inside the rack the trays reach each other over copper ([source](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/nvl72-ai-factory/latest/components.html)). Between neighboring racks, the signal can travel up to 7 meters over active electrical cables, a type of copper cable sold by firms such as Credo ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026043303/crdo-20260502.htm)). Longer links run on glass fiber, with an optical transceiver at each end. A laser in the sending transceiver turns the electrical signal into pulses of light, and a detector in the receiving one turns the pulses back into current.

The accelerators in an AI cluster exchange data over two networks that are not interchangeable. Scale-up connects anywhere from a few dozen accelerators to thousands closely enough that they can read each other's memory. It runs at terabytes per second. Scale-out joins those groups to one another at hundreds of gigabits per second. Scale-up speeds are quoted in bytes and scale-out speeds in bits, and a byte is eight bits. Each GPU in a GB300 NVL72 can send 900 gigabytes per second each way over the scale-up network, or 7,200 gigabits per second ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/co-packaged-optics-cpo-book-scaling)). Over the scale-out network it can send 800 gigabits per second ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/co-packaged-optics-cpo-book-scaling)). So each GPU has nine times as much bandwidth for scale-up as for scale-out, SemiAnalysis calculated in January 2026 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/co-packaged-optics-cpo-book-scaling)).

Scale-up bandwidth limits how efficiently a large model trains. A model too big for one accelerator is split across many. The parts that exchange data constantly stay inside the scale-up group, because they need its high bandwidth ([source](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/features/parallelisms.html)). Whole blocks of layers, the stages a model's calculation passes through in turn, can sit on different groups across the slower scale-out network ([source](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/features/parallelisms.html)).

## Variants and trade-offs

### Scale-up: NVLink, UALink and Google's torus

The designs differ in how many accelerators can share one pool of memory.

- **NVLink and NVSwitch.** Nvidia's NVLink bandwidth per GPU, counting both directions, went from 900 GB/s in NVLink 4 to 1,800 GB/s in NVLink 5 ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/nvlink/)). Across a whole Blackwell NVL72 rack, NVLink moves 130 TB/s ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/nvlink/)). Nvidia's Rubin launch release of January 2026 gives NVLink 6 as 3,600 GB/s per GPU and 260 TB/s for a Vera Rubin NVL72 rack ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/rubin-platform-ai-supercomputer)). As of October 2026, Nvidia's NVLink product page still lists preliminary figures of 3,000 GB/s and 216 TB/s ([source](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/nvlink/)).
- **UALink.** The open specification, from the UALink Consortium, runs 200 Gb/s per lane, and four lanes, which it calls a station, carry 800 Gb/s ([source](https://ualinkconsortium.org/blog/ualink-200g-1-0-specification-overview-802/)). It can address up to 1,024 endpoints, such as accelerators, in one group ([source](https://ualinkconsortium.org/blog/ualink-200g-1-0-specification-overview-802/)). AMD's Helios rack, launched in July 2026, joins 72 GPUs and runs UALink over Ethernet for its scale-up network ([source](https://newsroom.amd.com/news/aai-2026-helios-update/)).
- **Google's 3D torus.** Google wires 64 of its Ironwood TPU accelerators into each rack, called a cube ([source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/inside-the-ironwood-tpu-codesigned-ai-stack)). Inside the cube each chip links directly to six others, in a three-dimensional grid that Google calls a 3D torus ([source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/inside-the-ironwood-tpu-codesigned-ai-stack)). Optical circuit switches, which steer light from one fiber to another, join cubes into superpods of 9,216 chips and can route around a failed cube or link ([source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/inside-the-ironwood-tpu-codesigned-ai-stack)).

### Scale-out: InfiniBand versus Ethernet

Scale-out networks typically use InfiniBand or Ethernet, SemiAnalysis wrote in January 2026 ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/co-packaged-optics-cpo-book-scaling)). InfiniBand is a specialized network long used in supercomputers. But Ethernet switch capacity has grown quickly. Broadcom's Tomahawk 4 switched 25.6 Tbps on one chip in December 2019 ([source](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-ships-tomahawk-4-industrys-highest-bandwidth-ethernet)). Tomahawk 5 reached 51.2 Tbps in August 2022, and Broadcom said it had doubled the bandwidth about every two years since 2014 ([source](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-ships-tomahawk-5-industrys-highest-bandwidth-switch)). Tomahawk 6 doubled it again to 102.4 Tbps in June 2025 ([source](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-ships-tomahawk-6-worlds-first-1024-tbps-switch)). In June 2025 the Ultra Ethernet Consortium published its 1.0 specification, a set of Ethernet-based rules for communication in AI and supercomputing ([source](https://ultraethernet.org/ultra-ethernet-consortium-uec-launches-specification-1-0-transforming-ethernet-for-ai-and-hpc-at-scale/)). It covers how data is carried, how traffic jams are avoided and how one machine reads or writes another's memory directly ([source](https://ultraethernet.org/ultra-ethernet-consortium-uec-launches-specification-1-0-transforming-ethernet-for-ai-and-hpc-at-scale/)).

Nvidia sells switches and network cards for both InfiniBand and Ethernet. Its data center revenue, which counts its GPUs as well as its networking gear, reached $89.0 billion in the quarter to July 2026, up 117 percent from a year earlier ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)). In the quarter before, it credited its data center growth to Blackwell 300 products and to demand for its "InfiniBand, Spectrum-X Ethernet, and NVLink" solutions ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000052/nvda-20260426.htm)).

### Optics: pluggable, then co-packaged

Broadcom and Nvidia are moving the optics out of pluggable transceivers and into the switch chip's own package, a design called co-packaged optics. A cluster needs several transceivers, the modules that turn electricity into light and back, for each accelerator. Their power cost is one reason for the change. Broadcom says its Tomahawk 6 Davisson cuts the power used by optical links by 70 percent compared with traditional pluggable modules ([source](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-announces-tomahawkr-6-davisson-industrys-first-1024)). It keeps its lasers in field-replaceable modules ([source](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-announces-tomahawkr-6-davisson-industrys-first-1024)). Nvidia's Spectrum-X Photonics switches, announced in March 2025, have up to 512 ports of 800 gigabits per second each, and Nvidia says they use four times fewer lasers than traditional designs ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-spectrum-x-co-packaged-optics-networking-switches-ai-factories)). Nvidia builds them on its Spectrum-6 switch design ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/rubin-platform-ai-supercomputer)). In August 2026 Nvidia said its Spectrum-6 switch systems, with pluggable or co-packaged optics, were arriving at AI data centers around the world ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027)).

## Who makes it

Chinese makers of optical modules have built plants outside China. InnoLight has run a plant in Thailand since 2019, alongside its sites in Suzhou and Taiwan ([source](https://www.innolight.com/about)). The Thai subsidiary of Eoptolink, another Chinese module maker, lists sites in Chonburi and Rayong ([source](https://www.eoptolink.com/about-us/contacts)).

A US rule from July 2026 may reach optical transceivers made by firms on a US security list. The list is the Federal Communications Commission's Covered List of equipment and services judged to pose an unacceptable risk to US national security or to the security and safety of US persons ([source](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf#page=3)). Under its July 2026 order, the FCC no longer approves new devices for the US market if they contain logic-bearing hardware components made by firms on the list ([source](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf#page=6)). The order counts as logic-bearing any part that uses digital logic, from a single chip to a whole module, and leaves out parts with no digital function, such as wiring and housings ([source](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf#page=13)). Commenters in the record named optical transceivers as one such component ([source](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-50A1.pdf#page=10)). As of 16 October 2026, the Covered List did not include InnoLight ([source](https://www.fcc.gov/supplychain/coveredlist)).

China produces most of the world's indium, the raw material for indium phosphide lasers, and restricts its export. Indium phosphide is used in the high-speed laser diodes and photodetectors for optical communications ([source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-indium.pdf)). China accounted for 70 percent of world indium production in 2025 ([source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-indium.pdf)). In February 2025 China's Ministry of Commerce placed indium under new export restrictions ([source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-indium.pdf)).

AI data centers were straining supplies of the lasers inside optical modules and pushing up their prices, Nikkei Asia reported in May 2026 ([source](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/tech-asia/ai-demand-strains-supplies-of-lasers-fiber-and-other-optical-tech)). Nvidia had agreed in March 2026 to invest $2 billion in one laser maker, Lumentum, alongside a multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and rights to future capacity for advanced laser components ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-strategic-partnership-with-lumentum-to-develop-state-of-the-art-optics-technology)). Coherent, another laser maker, broke ground in Sherman, Texas, in June 2026 on an expansion of its factory there, which makes optical devices from indium phosphide ([source](https://www.coherent.com/news/press-releases/a-chip-letter-of-intent-for-50m-to-expand-world-leading-manufacturing-facility-for-ai-infrastructure)).

Two American firms, Broadcom and Marvell, sell both custom AI chips and Ethernet switch chips. In its annual report for fiscal 2025, Broadcom said its AI chips include Ethernet switch chips as well as custom accelerators, which it builds to each customer's specifications ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016825000121/avgo-20251102.htm)). Its AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, up 221 percent from a year earlier ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000173016826000076/avgo-08022026x8kxex99.htm)). Marvell also builds custom AI chips to each customer's specifications and sells Ethernet switch chips, its annual report for fiscal 2026 says ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835632/000183563226000011/mrvl-20260131.htm)). Marvell's five-year agreement with Amazon Web Services, announced in December 2024, covers custom AI chips alongside the optical and networking parts that go with them ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835632/000183563224000193/final2024_12x02xmarvell-.htm)).

Taiwanese firms do much of the physical assembly. Foxconn booked NT$8.1 trillion of revenue in 2025, up 18 percent, and cloud and networking was its largest product category in the fourth quarter ([source](https://www.honhai.com/en-us/press-center/press-releases/latest-news/1978)). In the same results release, its chairman called Foxconn the world's largest AI server provider ([source](https://www.honhai.com/en-us/press-center/press-releases/latest-news/1978)).

Some assembly is moving to the United States. In April 2025 Nvidia said it was building supercomputer manufacturing plants in Texas, with Foxconn in Houston and Wistron in Dallas ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-manufacture-american-made-ai-supercomputers-us/)). It expected mass production to ramp up in the following 12 to 15 months ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-manufacture-american-made-ai-supercomputers-us/)). In August 2026 Nvidia said Wistron's new plant in Fort Worth, Texas, near Dallas, was producing its GB300 superchips, which pair Nvidia's Grace processor with its Blackwell Ultra GPUs ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-and-partners-build-in-america-for-america/)). It said Foxconn was building a factory in Houston to make Nvidia's AI systems ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-and-partners-build-in-america-for-america/)).

## The chokepoint

Many firms can build the racks. In October 2025 Nvidia said more than 50 makers of systems and components were gearing up for the Vera Rubin NVL72, which follows MGX, Nvidia's standard design for trays and racks ([source](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/gigawatt-ai-factories-ocp-vera-rubin)). But in its filing for the quarter to July 2026, Nvidia said the complexity of producing its data center systems had caused production delays ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).

Four inputs to the rack are harder to source elsewhere.

- **Indium phosphide laser capacity.** Nvidia has secured rights to future capacity from laser makers. Alongside its Lumentum deal, in March 2026 Nvidia agreed to invest $2 billion in Coherent ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-coherent-announce-strategic-partnership-to-develop-optics-technology-to-scale-next-generation-data-center-architecture)). The deal also includes a multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and rights to future access and capacity for Coherent's advanced laser and optical networking products ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-coherent-announce-strategic-partnership-to-develop-optics-technology-to-scale-next-generation-data-center-architecture)).
- **Indium phosphide substrates.** China added indium phosphide substrates, the wafers the lasers are grown on, to its export control list on 4 February 2025 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). AXT, a US-listed maker of these wafers, does all its manufacturing in China ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). In its filing for the quarter to June 2026, AXT called the need for Chinese export permits the most significant challenge it faces ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). It said it could not estimate when it would resume shipping the wafers to the United States ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). The same filing names Sumitomo and JX, both Japanese firms, as AXT's competitors in indium phosphide ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)).
- **NVSwitch.** Nvidia's annual report for fiscal 2026 lists NVLink interconnects and switches among its own networking products ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). UALink is an open standard designed for accelerator communications within one scale-up group ([source](https://ualinkconsortium.org/blog/ualink-200g-1-0-specification-overview-802/)). Since May 2025 Nvidia's NVLink Fusion program has let other firms' custom chips use NVLink, including CPUs that Fujitsu and Qualcomm plan to connect to Nvidia GPUs ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-nvlink-fusion-semi-custom-ai-infrastructure-partner-ecosystem)).
- **High-layer-count circuit boards.** TTM Technologies, an American circuit board maker, says its circuit board competitors are mostly based in China and Taiwan ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1116942/000119312526051976/ttmi-20251229.htm)). Nitto Boseki, a Japanese glass fiber maker, makes NE-glass, a glass cloth that absorbs less signal, for the boards in data center switches and routers ([source](https://www.nittobo.co.jp/eng/ir/pdf/Nittobo_Integrated-Report_2025.pdf#page=7)). See [Substrates and PCBs](#substrates-and-pcbs).

Optical module assembly can move, as InnoLight's high-volume plant in Thailand, opened in 2019, shows ([source](https://www.innolight.com/about)).

## Takeaways

In March 2026 Nvidia agreed to invest $2 billion in the laser maker Lumentum, alongside a multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and rights to future capacity for advanced laser components ([source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-strategic-partnership-with-lumentum-to-develop-state-of-the-art-optics-technology)). Indium phosphide is used in high-speed lasers for optical communications, and China produced 70 percent of the world's indium in 2025 ([source](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-indium.pdf)).

On 4 February 2025 China added indium phosphide substrates, the wafers the lasers are grown on, to its export control list ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). AXT, a US-listed maker of those wafers, does all its manufacturing in China ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)). In its filing for the quarter to June 2026, AXT said it could not estimate when it would resume shipping the wafers to the United States ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926027677/axti20260630_10q.htm)).
