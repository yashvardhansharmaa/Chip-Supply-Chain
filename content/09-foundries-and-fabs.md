# Foundries and Fabs

TSMC calls a plant a GIGAFAB once it can run more than 100,000 wafers a month, each a 300 mm disc, twelve inches across ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/gigafab)). It operates six of them, and every one is in Taiwan ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)).

## How it works

A foundry is a chip factory, or fab, that makes chips for other companies. Those companies send in their designs, and the foundry runs them all through the same building on the same tools. The building costs tens of billions of dollars, and the air inside is cleaner than in an operating room. Even so, each wafer, the silicon disc the chips are built on, moves between hundreds of machines over several months inside sealed boxes and never touches that air. No chip company sells enough of one product to fill such a factory on its own, so chip companies share a handful of them. TSMC runs the biggest, and all six of its largest plants are in Taiwan, so a political crisis over Taiwan would affect the whole chip supply.

Inside, a chip is built up in layers, and the same loop of steps repeats for each one. Wafers travel in a lot, a batch of 25 in a sealed pod called a FOUP that only a machine opens. An overhead track carries the pod from tool to tool.

At each tool a robot lifts one wafer out and the tool does its one job: depositing a film, printing a pattern, etching it, cleaning it or measuring it. Then the robot puts the wafer back. The pod moves on to the next tool, often across the building. When a layer is finished, the loop starts again with the next one. A chip takes hundreds of those visits, so a wafer spends months inside.

The pod keeps the wafer clean for all of that time because the wafer never meets the room's air. That air is also filtered until it carries almost no dust.

Every design in the building runs on the same tools because each tool is set by a recipe, the list of gases, temperatures and times for one step. A foundry offers each customer the same list of recipes on shared machines. The tools run day and night, because an idle tool earns nothing. The finished wafer is tested (see [Test and assembly](#test-and-assembly)), then cut and packaged.

TSMC, founded in 1987, pioneered the pure-play model, in which a foundry makes only its customers' products ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/aboutTSMC/company_profile)). In 2023 the Boston Consulting Group (BCG) estimated that a new fab for chips at 2 nm and below, completed in 2026, would cost $35 to $43 billion to own over ten years ([source](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/navigating-the-semiconductor-manufacturing-costs)). That is 33 to 66 percent above 2023 costs ([source](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/navigating-the-semiconductor-manufacturing-costs)). In March 2026 the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) told the US Senate that building and equipping a leading-edge fab takes as much as $20 to $25 billion of capital ([source](https://www.epw.senate.gov/public/_cache/files/a/c/ac4552ff-551b-40d5-bea6-19dd17a1cfea/1F7BCD91016CBE352434701BE58395874BEA6A892A23ABEE1D7214EC924EC0EB.03-04-2026-isaacs-testimony.pdf)).

Together, the plants TSMC and its subsidiaries run had capacity for more than 17 million wafers a year in 2025, counted as if each were the standard 12-inch size ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)). The six largest plants, called gigafabs, had a combined capacity of more than 13 million ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/gigafab)). All six are in Taiwan ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)). The other plants are five smaller fabs in Taiwan, two in China, two in the United States and one in Japan ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)).

## Variants and trade-offs

### Pure-play foundries

TSMC and UMC, both based in Taiwan, make chips only for other companies. Chips at 7 nm and below, meaning the newest processes, were 77 percent of TSMC's wafer revenue in the second quarter of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)). That share split into 33 percent at 5 nm, 30 at 3 nm, 11 at 7 nm and 3 at 2 nm ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)). TSMC's revenue for the quarter was $40.20 billion, at a 67.7 percent gross margin ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)).

### Integrated device makers turned foundry

Intel and Samsung design and sell their own chips and rent out capacity too. Renting out capacity is harder for them, because customers dislike handing designs to a competitor and the company's own products take the best capacity. Intel Foundry reported $5.8 billion of segment revenue in the second quarter of 2026 and an operating loss of $2.089 billion ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1776/intel-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)). Only $293 million of that revenue came from outside customers ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000157/intc-20260627.htm)). Intel says substantially all of its foundry business still makes Intel's own products ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000157/intc-20260627.htm)).

### National champions

China's SMIC and Hua Hong and Japan's Rapidus have government backing. SMIC's revenue rose 16.2 percent to $9.327 billion in 2025, at a 21 percent gross margin ([source](https://www.smics.com/en/site/news_read/7951)). Its lines ran 93.5 percent full, on monthly capacity above a million wafers counted in the smaller 8-inch size ([source](https://www.smics.com/en/site/news_read/7951)). SMIC cannot buy an EUV scanner, yet it makes 7 nm-class chips on its N+2 and N+3 processes by printing the tightest layers in several passes on older deep-ultraviolet scanners ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/steel-smic-n3-teardown)).

Georgetown University's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) reported in June 2024 that Huawei's Ascend 910B AI chip is believed to be made on N+2 ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/pushing-the-limits-huaweis-ai-chip-tests-u-s-export-controls/)). It also reported that SMIC's limited 7 nm capacity forced Huawei to choose between its most advanced phone chips and its AI chips ([source](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/pushing-the-limits-huaweis-ai-chip-tests-u-s-export-controls/)). But the research firm SemiAnalysis judged in April 2025 that most 910B and 910C chips were made on TSMC's 7 nm process, from wafers Huawei bought through another company ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ai-cloudmatrix-384-chinas-answer-to-nvidia-gb200-nvl72)).

In February 2026 Rapidus raised 267.6 billion yen from the Japanese government and 32 companies to reach 2 nm mass production by 2027 ([source](https://www.rapidus.inc/en/news_topics/information/rapidus-secures-267-6-billion-yen-in-funding-from-japan-government-and-private-sector-companies/)).

### Mature-node capacity

Most wafers come off older lines. These make power devices, analog chips and microcontrollers, and they compete on price. In January 2025 the industry association SEMI forecast that foundry capacity at all nodes would rise from 11.3 million wafers a month in 2024 to a record 12.6 million in 2025, counted in the 8-inch size ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/semi-press-release/eighteen-new-semiconductor-fabs-to-start-construction-in-2025-semi-reports)). Its June 2024 forecast had credited much of that growth to Intel's new foundry business and China's expansion ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/news-media-press-releases/semi-press-releases/global-semiconductor-fab-capacity-projected-to-expand-6%25-in-2024-and-7%25-in-2025-semi-reports)). But in July 2025 Intel scaled back its own fab plans, saying it would no longer go ahead with planned projects in Germany and Poland and would slow construction in Ohio ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1745/intel-reports-second-quarter-2025-financial-results)).

## Capacity and cost

In March 2025 TSMC added $100 billion to the $65 billion it had already committed in Phoenix, for three more fabs, two packaging plants and an R&D center ([source](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3210)). In July 2026 it added another $100 billion for four more fabs at 2 nm or below, taking its total investment in Arizona to $265 billion across 12 facilities ([source](https://www.phoenix.gov/newsroom/ced-news/tsmc-announces-additional--100-billion-investment-in-arizona.html)). All of TSMC's gigafabs are still in Taiwan ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)).

Some customers pay TSMC in advance to hold capacity at its fabs ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000162828026025362/tsm-20251231.htm)). These deposits stood at NT$189.9 billion at the end of 2025, down from NT$291.1 billion a year earlier ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000162828026025362/tsm-20251231.htm)). In 2025 the four largest AI chip designers used around 90 percent of the world's CoWoS packaging capacity and HBM memory supply, against only 12 percent of advanced logic production ([source](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-chip-supply-chain-constraints)). See [Advanced packaging](#advanced-packaging).

## Who makes it

The US government owns part of Intel. In August 2025 the government bought 433.3 million newly issued Intel shares at $20.47 each, or $8.9 billion in all, for a 9.9 percent stake ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)). It paid with $5.7 billion of CHIPS Act grants that Intel had not yet received and $3.2 billion from the Secure Enclave program ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)).

The stake is passive, with no board seat ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)). The government agreed to vote with Intel's board on matters that need shareholder approval, with limited exceptions ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)). The deal also gave the government a five-year warrant, a right to buy a further 5 percent of Intel at $20 a share ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)). The government can use that right only if Intel's ownership of its foundry business falls below 51 percent ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1748/intel-and-trump-administration-reach-historic-agreement-to)).

In 2026 Intel began high-volume manufacturing of some of its Panther Lake processors using ASML's High-NA extreme ultraviolet scanners ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1776/intel-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)). It also put its 18A-P process into risk production, the trial runs a process goes through before it is signed off ([source](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1776/intel-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)).

In 2022 Samsung broke ground in Taylor, Texas, on a plant with an initial minimum investment of $17 billion, split into $6 billion for buildings and $11 billion for equipment ([source](https://semiconductor.samsung.com/sas/company/taylor/)). In December 2024 the Commerce Department awarded Samsung up to $4.745 billion of CHIPS Act funding, to support more than $37 billion of planned investment in Central Texas ([source](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/12/biden-harris-administration-announces-chips-incentives-award-samsung)). That plan covers two new leading-edge logic fabs and a research fab in Taylor, and an expansion of Samsung's existing plant in Austin ([source](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/12/biden-harris-administration-announces-chips-incentives-award-samsung)).

## The chokepoint

In 2022 South Korea and Taiwan held almost all of the world's logic capacity below 10 nm ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). The United States had almost no capacity below 10 nm that year ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). In May 2024 SIA and BCG projected that the US share of that capacity would reach 28 percent by 2032 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). They also projected that new fab investment worldwide would reach around $2.3 trillion from 2024 to 2032 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)).

Across all nodes, old and new, SEMI forecast in June 2024 that China would have installed capacity of 10.1 million wafers a month in 2025, the most of any region ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/news-media-press-releases/semi-press-releases/global-semiconductor-fab-capacity-projected-to-expand-6%25-in-2024-and-7%25-in-2025-semi-reports)). It forecast 5.8 million for Taiwan, out of a world total of 33.7 million, all counted in the 8-inch size ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/news-media-press-releases/semi-press-releases/global-semiconductor-fab-capacity-projected-to-expand-6%25-in-2024-and-7%25-in-2025-semi-reports)). In the third-quarter 2026 update of its World Fab Forecast, SEMI projected that China would keep the largest installed capacity in 2026 and 2027 ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/products-services/market-data/world-fab-forecast)). Little of China's capacity is advanced ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)).

{{figure:capacity-by-region}}

Chip production in Taiwan faces three risks:

- **Seismic.** Taiwan sits on an active plate boundary, and after an earthquake in January 2025 TSMC had to scrap some wafers in process ([source](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3204)).
- **Power.** A gigafab is a large industrial load on a small island grid.
- **Political.** Taiwan held 69 percent of the world's logic capacity below 10 nm in 2022 ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). In December 2025 SemiAnalysis put Taiwan's share of the world's advanced-node capacity at over 90 percent, though it did not say which node sizes count as advanced ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tsmc-overseas-fabs-a-success)). So a blockade or conflict would cut most of that output off from the world market at once. See [Geopolitics](#geopolitics).

Building capacity outside Taiwan is slow, and TSMC has yet to build a gigafab anywhere else ([source](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/manufacturing/fab_capacity)).

## Key evaluation criteria

- **Leading-edge share.** The share of revenue from the newest processes. At TSMC, 7 nm and below made up 77 percent of wafer revenue in the second quarter of 2026 ([source](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)).
- **Yield at ramp.** How many good chips a new process gives in its first year. If too few work, the process loses money and has to be subsidized.
- **Ten-year cost of ownership.** $35 to $43 billion for a new 2 nm-class fab finished in 2026, in BCG's 2023 estimate, and rising ([source](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/navigating-the-semiconductor-manufacturing-costs)).
- **Utilization.** How full the lines run. SMIC's lines ran 93.5 percent full in 2025, at a 21 percent gross margin ([source](https://www.smics.com/en/site/news_read/7951)).
- **Customer concentration.** A foundry owned by a chipmaker competes with its own customers, so some of them will not trust it with a flagship design.
- **Geographic exposure.** How much of a buyer's supply comes from one island, with a single power grid and a single fault system.

## Takeaways

In 2022 Taiwan held 69 percent of the world's capacity for advanced logic chips, those below 10 nanometers, and the United States almost none ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). In July 2026 TSMC raised its planned investment in Arizona to $265 billion across 12 facilities ([source](https://www.phoenix.gov/newsroom/ced-news/tsmc-announces-additional--100-billion-investment-in-arizona.html)).

The industry association SEMI projected in 2026 that China would keep the world's largest installed fab capacity in 2026 and 2027 ([source](https://www.semi.org/en/products-services/market-data/world-fab-forecast)). Little of China's capacity is advanced ([source](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Report_Emerging-Resilience-in-the-Semiconductor-Supply-Chain.pdf)). China's SMIC cannot buy an EUV scanner, the machine that prints the finest layers, yet it makes 7 nm-class chips by printing those layers in several passes on older deep-ultraviolet scanners ([source](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/steel-smic-n3-teardown)).
